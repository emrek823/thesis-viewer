## Overview

Pharma commercial infrastructure is fragmenting for the first time in a decade. Veeva owns 80-90% CRM market share and grew 16%+ in FY2026 to $3B run rate, targeting $6B by 2030—but the mandatory Vault CRM migration (2025-2030) forces 200+ pharma companies to make active platform decisions for the first time in years. Salesforce re-entered with Life Sciences Cloud (September 2025), signing Takeda, AstraZeneca, Novartis, and 70+ customers by August 2025, partnering with IQVIA for data advantage. This is the first credible CRM threat in a decade. Meanwhile, field force economics are structurally broken: reps declined 20-30% post-COVID and are not returning, 85%+ of launches through 2026 are nano/micro-launches under $500M, and MSLs now rated more valuable than sales reps by 58% of HCPs. The $140B outsourced biopharma market faces a $236B revenue cliff from patent expiration.

The opportunity is across the commercial stack, from pre-launch to market access to medical affairs to direct-to-patient to content marketing. Nano-launches cannot support the old model—$2-3M agency contracts and 20-person brand teams do not pencil for sub-$500M drugs. Content bottleneck is quantified: $50K per email, 50-60 day MLR cycles, 27 hours to fact-check one detail aid. Startups that deliver 10x cost efficiency in any layer of the commercial stack have a structural buyer in every pharma company facing this math. The buyers are brand marketers (constrained to sub-$500K budgets on nano-launches), medical affairs (needing commercial firewall), and commercial ops (managing split Veeva/Salesforce ecosystems). Incumbents can't fill the gap: Veeva optimizes for enterprise complexity, not nano-launch economics; agencies charge $2-3M per brand that nano-launches can't afford; Salesforce is rebuilding but lacks workflow depth.

## Bull Case

**1. Nano-launch economics force structural change**
85%+ of launches through 2026 are nano/micro-launches under $500M—"what pharma has done before will not work in this paradigm." These drugs cannot support $2-3M agency contracts, 20-person brand teams, or large field forces. Cost compression is mandatory, not optional. The math is simple: a $300M drug cannot fund a $3M agency contract and a 50-person field force.
> "In the near future, more than 85% of launches through 2026 are expected to be nano- and micro-launches (under $500 million in sales)... What pharma has done before will not work in this paradigm." — Industry research

**2. Content bottleneck is quantified and massive**
MLR review cycles run 50-60 days standard; simple emails take 2 months and $50K. Agencies charge $2-3M per brand annually for 15-20 person teams. AI-native platforms demonstrate 86% cost reduction and 12x time-to-market improvement. Pre-MLR compliance checking alone replaces the 27-hour fact-check cycle.
> "Simple email: 2 months, $50K... AI platforms: minutes. 15x cost savings vs agencies." — Industry analysis

**3. Rep model is broken at the physics level**
Only 50% of providers are fully accessible. HCPs receive 1.4 touchpoints every working hour from pharma manufacturers. 58% of HCPs rate MSL interactions more valuable than sales rep calls. Field forces declined 20-30% post-COVID and aren't returning. The rep-centric "interruption model" doesn't work, but pharma's organizational structure still assumes it does—creating lag for AI-native alternatives to capture budget.
> "The rep-centered 'interruption model' isn't effective now, nor will it be in the future... 1.4 touchpoints every working hour from pharma manufacturers." — Industry research

**4. Platform instability creates switching window**
Mandatory Vault CRM migration (2025-2030) forces 200+ pharma companies to make active platform decisions. Salesforce signed AstraZeneca and Novartis (December 2025), Takeda (May 2025), 70+ customers total. This is the first credible CRM alternative in a decade. When platform choice becomes an active decision rather than default, every adjacent layer of the stack becomes contestable.

## Bear Case

**1. Veeva PromoMats AI may prove "good enough"**
Quick Check Agent and Content Agent launched December 2025, claiming 75% cycle time reduction for MLR review. If bundled AI proves sufficient, standalone content platforms lose their wedge. Moderna is early customer. Veeva has 80-90% PromoMats market share and can distribute AI features to entire installed base overnight. Threshold to watch: if 50%+ of PromoMats customers adopt AI features within 18 months, content window closes.
> "We expect faster approvals and shorter cycle times, but the real key metric is delivering better content." — Jason Benagh, Moderna

**2. Salesforce may capture the fragmentation**
Life Sciences Cloud plus IQVIA data partnership is formidable. If Salesforce consolidates the anti-Veeva market into a new duopoly, startups building cross-platform tools lose their reason to exist. Pharma CRM market projected $6.88B by 2035 at 8.4% CAGR—big enough for duopoly, not fragmentation. Threshold: if Salesforce signs 5+ top-20 pharma by 2027, re-consolidation is underway.

**3. AI content hallucination creates regulatory chill**
FDA issued 100+ warning letters in 2025 targeting AI-generated content. Non-deterministic models can hallucinate unsubstantiated claims and introduce off-label risks. Human-in-the-loop remains mandatory. Mid-level marketers remain "extremely skeptical that gen AI could help with MLR" due to hallucination concerns. If AI content faces systematic FDA rejection, the efficiency gains evaporate.

**4. Customer dissatisfaction doesn't translate to churn**
Despite frustration with Veeva pricing and roadmap prioritization, customers still say "there are no good competitive options" for CTMS/eTMF. Pharma procurement cycles are 9-18 months. Startups may run out of runway before landing enough MSAs to prove unit economics. The switching window may be narrower than it appears—talk doesn't become action.
> "Veeva is one example... they're not trying to lose their market share. They are continuously working hand in hand with their clients to build whatever they need." — Industry operator

## Startup Opportunities

**1. AI-Native Content Engine for Nano-Launches (Stage: Proven)**
- Why this follows: Content bottleneck is quantified ($50K/email, 2-month cycle) and nano-launches force digital-first commercial models. Agencies cannot serve sub-$500M drugs economically.
- Wedge: Pre-MLR compliance checking with 86% cost reduction, then expand to full content creation.
- The edge: Claims ontology (brand-specific regulatory knowledge codified in software) creates switching costs incumbents cannot replicate quickly. 15x cost savings vs agencies. PharmD/PhD domain knowledge encoded in software, not generic LLM fine-tuning.
- Buyers: Brand marketers ($2-3M budget per brand on blockbusters, sub-$500K on nano-launches), marketing ops, medical affairs
- Risk: Veeva PromoMats AI adoption exceeds 50% in 18 months

**2. Engagement Measurement and Attribution (Stage: Emerging)**
- Why this follows: As spend shifts from reps to digital, pharma must prove ROI per channel per brand. Field insights cannot be integrated into Veeva ("reps keep own spreadsheets"). 94% of pharma exploring direct-to-patient—all need attribution.
- Wedge: Cross-platform attribution that works across both Veeva and Salesforce ecosystems.
- The edge: CRM-agnostic measurement creates value regardless of which platform wins. Proprietary data on content performance compounds over time.
- Buyers: Commercial analytics, brand directors, CFOs demanding marketing accountability
- Risk: Veeva-IQVIA data partnership closes the measurement gap

**3. Medical Affairs Content Platform (Stage: Emerging)**
- Why this follows: Compliance separation from commercial creates structural demand. Publication management not well-served by Veeva commercial tools.
- Wedge: Medical affairs content review, publication management, MSL enablement—deliberately not touching commercial.
- The edge: Firewall value proposition. "Most companies simply don't want to use the same platform because the systems are entirely different." Being medical-only is the feature.
- Buyers: Medical affairs leadership, compliance, regulatory
- Risk: Veeva launches dedicated medical affairs module with clear separation

**4. Cross-Platform Integration Layer (Stage: Early)**
- Why this follows: Salesforce entry means pharma will have split CRM ecosystems for first time. Data unification across platforms becomes hard requirement.
- Wedge: Workflow orchestration and data integration across Veeva and Salesforce instances.
- The edge: Neutral infrastructure that both platforms need but neither will build. Abbott has 8 divisions with different platform needs—every global pharma has this problem.
- Buyers: IT/IS, commercial ops, regulatory operations
- Risk: One platform wins decisively and integration becomes unnecessary
