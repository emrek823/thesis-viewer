# Pharma Commercial Stack Fragments After Decade of Veeva Dominance

## Overview

Pharma commercial infrastructure is fragmenting for the first time in a decade. Veeva owns 80-90% CRM market share and grew 16%+ in FY2026 to $3B run rate, targeting $6B by 2030—but the mandatory Vault CRM migration (2025-2030) forces 200+ pharma companies to make active platform decisions for the first time in years. Salesforce re-entered with Life Sciences Cloud (September 2025), signing Takeda and 70+ customers by August 2025, partnering with IQVIA for data advantage. This is the first credible CRM threat in a decade. Meanwhile, field force economics are structurally broken: reps declined 20-30% post-COVID and are not returning, 85%+ of launches through 2026 are nano/micro-launches under $500M, and MSLs now rated more valuable than sales reps by 58% of HCPs. The $140B outsourced biopharma market faces $236B revenue cliff from patent expiration.

The opportunity is in the content layer where MLR review cycles run 50-60 days at $50K per simple email. Agencies charge $2-3M per brand annually for 15-20 person teams; a single detail aid takes 27 hours to fact-check. AI-native platforms demonstrate 86% cost reduction and 12x time-to-market improvement (Solstice: $2.3M ARR in 4 months, 14x ACV expansion within top-10 pharma, replacing Omnicom and Indegene). Veeva launched PromoMats AI in December 2025 (Quick Check Agent, Content Agent) with Moderna as early customer—but bundled "good enough" may not match specialized depth. Compliance separation between medical and commercial creates additional wedge: pharma companies want different platforms to prove firewall, making Veeva's commercial dominance a liability for medical affairs content. The window is 2-3 years before incumbents close the gap or startups reach $50M+ ARR escape velocity.

## Bull Case

**Field force economics structurally broken.** Field forces declined 20-30% post-COVID and are not returning. 85%+ of launches through 2026 are nano/micro-launches under $500M — "what pharma has done before will not work in this paradigm." Medical science liaisons are replacing traditional reps as primary HCP touchpoint: 58% of HCPs rate MSL interactions more valuable than sales rep calls.
> "The rep-centered 'interruption model' isn't effective now, nor will it be in the future... 1.4 touchpoints every working hour from pharma manufacturers." — [[2026-01-23-pharmas-commercial-model-of-tomorrow-starts-today]]

**Content bottleneck quantified at $50K/email, 2-month cycles.** MLR review cycles run 50-60 days standard with 3x content volume increase overwhelming traditional workflows. Agencies charge $2-3M per brand annually for 15-20 person teams. AI-native platforms demonstrate 86% cost reduction and 12x time-to-market improvement with single engagement leads replacing entire agency teams.
> "Simple email: 2 months, $50K... Platform: minutes. $2.3M ARR in 4 months; 14x ACV within a top-10 pharma in 3 months." — [[solstice-series-a-deck-jan2026]]

**Platform lock-in is massive but cracking.** Gilead spends $10-12M/year on clinical, $15M on quality, $12-15M on regulatory via Veeva. Changing CRM systems with 2,000-person field forces requires 1+ month training. Yet Gilead sees Veeva "heading in the same direction" as Oracle before losing share — prioritizing CRO needs over sponsors, limiting customization, aggressive pricing. The mandatory Vault CRM migration by 2030 forces 200+ pharma companies to make active platform decisions for the first time in years.
> "We're paying for technology and services we don't fully utilize... the sponsor's voice is not being heard." — [[tegus_veeva-systems-inc_86838_senior-director-head-of-clinic-54a485e7]]

**Veeva-commercial separation creates firewall opportunity.** Pharma companies increasingly want different platforms for medical vs. commercial to prove separation for compliance. Veeva's dominance in promotional review (90% market share) makes it unattractive for medical affairs content. "Most companies simply don't want to use the same platform because the systems are entirely different" — this creates wedge for medical-side content and publication platforms.
> "If you can very clearly show that medical and commercial, all things medical and all things commercial are separate from one another... that's the best way to defend against any accusations." — [[tegus_veeva-systems-inc_63446_former-associate-partner-at-zs-64cb2bdb]]

## Bear Case

**Veeva PromoMats AI launched Dec 2025 with real capability.** Quick Check Agent and Content Agent claim 75% cycle time reduction for MLR review. If bundled AI proves "good enough," standalone content platforms lose their wedge. Veeva has 80-90% CRM market share, grew 16%+ in FY2026 to $3B run rate, and targets $6B by 2030.
> "We expect faster approvals and shorter cycle times, but the real key metric is delivering better content." — Jason Benagh, Global Marketing Operations Director, Moderna, [[2026-01-23-veeva-ai-for-promomats-veeva]]

**Salesforce re-entered with real firepower.** Life Sciences Cloud launched September 2025; Takeda signed May 2025; AstraZeneca and Novartis selected Agentforce December 2025; 70+ industry-leading customers by August 2025; IQVIA partnership provides data advantage. This is the first credible CRM threat in a decade. Pharma CRM market projected to reach $6.88B by 2035 at 8.4% CAGR.

**AI content hallucination risk creates regulatory chill.** FDA issued 100+ warning letters in 2025 targeting AI-generated content. Non-deterministic models can hallucinate unsubstantiated claims and introduce off-label risks. Human-in-the-loop remains mandatory. Mid-level marketers remain "extremely skeptical that gen AI could help with MLR" due to hallucination concerns.

**Customer dissatisfaction doesn't translate to churn.** Despite frustration with Veeva pricing (sponsors "pay twice") and roadmap prioritization, Gilead still says "there are no good competitive options" for CTMS/eTMF. Pharma procurement cycles are glacial at 9-18 months. AI-native startups may run out of runway before landing enough MSAs to prove unit economics.
> "Veeva is one example... they're not trying to lose their market share. They are continuously working hand in hand with their clients to build whatever they need." — [[tegus_veeva-systems-inc_63446_former-associate-partner-at-zs-64cb2bdb]]

**What would have to be true for you to be wrong?** Two scenarios kill this thesis. First, Veeva's PromoMats AI proves sufficient for content creation (not just MLR workflow) and pharma procurement defaults to the incumbent stack rather than evaluating AI-native alternatives — bundled "good enough" beats specialized depth. Second, Salesforce fails to consolidate beyond initial wins (Takeda, AstraZeneca, Novartis), Veeva's monopoly holds without innovation pressure, and the entire stack remains locked in a single platform that adds AI features incrementally. In either scenario, startups never reach the $50M+ ARR needed to establish switching costs before incumbents close the gap.

## Startup Opportunities

**1. Full-Stack Pharma Content Engine (Stage: Proven)**
- Why: Content bottleneck is quantified ($50K/email, 2-month cycle) and nano-launches force digital-first commercial models. Veeva CRM growth is capped, leaving content execution as greenfield.
- Wedge: MLR pre-screening with 86% cost reduction, then expand to full omnichannel content orchestration. Claims ontology (brand-specific regulatory knowledge codified in software) creates switching costs incumbents cannot replicate quickly.
- The edge: PharmD/PhD domain knowledge encoded in software, not generic LLM fine-tuning. Pre-MLR screening rated 4/5 value by Merck director; modular content + business rules enables automation. Agency replacement at 15x cost savings.
- Buyers: Brand marketers ($2-3M budget per brand), marketing ops, medical affairs
- Risk: Veeva PromoMats AI adoption exceeds 50% in 18 months

**2. Pharma Engagement Measurement and Attribution (Stage: Emerging)**
- Why: As spend shifts from reps to digital, pharma must prove ROI per channel per brand. Direct-to-patient strategies require measuring script lift from digital engagement.
- Wedge: Connect content delivery to prescription impact across both Veeva and Salesforce ecosystems.
- The edge: CRM-agnostic attribution that works regardless of which platform wins. Field insights cannot be integrated into Veeva ("reps keep own spreadsheets").
- Buyers: Commercial analytics, brand directors, CFOs demanding marketing accountability
- Risk: Veeva-IQVIA data partnership closes the measurement gap

**3. Cross-Platform Integration Layer (Stage: Early)**
- Why: Salesforce entry means pharma will have split CRM ecosystems for first time. Data unification across platforms becomes a hard requirement.
- Wedge: Data integration and workflow orchestration across Veeva and Salesforce instances.
- The edge: Neutral infrastructure that both platforms need but neither will build. Every pharma company operates globally with different systems by market; Abbott has 8 divisions with different needs.
- Buyers: IT/IS, commercial ops, regulatory operations
- Risk: One platform wins decisively and integration becomes unnecessary

**4. Medical Affairs Content Platform (Stage: Emerging)**
- Why: Compliance separation from commercial creates demand for medical-side platforms. Publication management not well-served by Veeva commercial tools. "If you're already on the medical side, there's less of a need for a firewall between medical and earlier development phases."
- Wedge: Medical affairs content review, publication management, MSL enablement — deliberately not touching commercial.
- The edge: Firewall value proposition: "the system we're using for all publication activities sits within medical, is run by medical, is owned by medical."
- Buyers: Medical affairs leadership, compliance, regulatory
- Risk: Veeva launches dedicated medical affairs module with clear separation

## Open Questions

**Will Veeva PromoMats AI adoption exceed 50% of existing customers within 18 months?** Stakes: If yes, content window closes faster than startups can scale to $50M ARR. Watch for Moderna, Bayer, GSK adoption rates and whether they expand beyond pilot.

**Does Salesforce sign 3+ additional top-20 pharma by end of 2027?** Stakes: Validates competitive CRM market and creates the split ecosystem that justifies integration layer startups. Current trajectory: 70+ customers but unclear how many are top-20.

**Do AI-native content platforms achieve regulatory approval rates equal to or better than agency-produced content?** Stakes: If FDA enforcement increases materially against AI content, the thesis stalls. Watch for FDA guidance on AI-generated promotional materials.
