# New Thesis

***Where does value accrue as AI enters healthcare risk?***
## Overview
There is opportunity between the blunt underwriting mechanisms of incumbents  arbitrage between precise and imprecise underwriting is collapsing. 
The gap is enormous: in pharmacy alone, WriteWise found a \$12.2M spread on a single employer group between what the incumbent PBM charged and the true cost. In MA, UnitedHealth, CVS, and Centene collectively mispriced \~\$19B in medical costs in a single year — UNH alone missed by \$6.5B, with Tim Noel admitting "our pricing assumptions were well short of actual medical costs." Indigo auto-underwrites 20% of malpractice submissions while legacy carriers still price OB in Florida at \~\$120K/year regardless of individual history. The resolution gap between how incumbents price and how AI can price is the entire margin.
The companies capturing it all converge on the same architecture: they own both the pricing model AND the clinical operation that produces the outcome the model predicted. Neither works without the other. Briggs put it precisely: "patient selection (underwriting) + post-procedure compliance" are the two differentiators — and the best companies collapse them into one system. Privia confirms this at scale: physician-led governance + actuarial underwriting + clinical operations drove 38.8% EBITDA growth to \$125.5M across 1.54M attributed lives. Carrum bundles surgical episodes, guarantees a price, selects the surgeon, and manages the episode — 0.8% knee readmission vs 2.7% national average. The readmission delta IS the underwriting edge.
We see three layers where AI enters healthcare risk, and most companies are building at the wrong one.

**Dashboards** are where most healthcare AI companies get stuck. ACO analytics, MLR decomposition, denial pattern visualization — useful but commoditized. The intelligence sits in a pane of glass that a human must act on, and the vendor captures \$5-15 PMPM in SaaS fees regardless of whether anyone does. This is the layer where incumbents (Milliman, Optum Advisory) already operate and where AI-native startups compete on UX, not structure.
**Workflow integration** is the transition layer — embedding predictions into clinical and operational decisions in real time. Bluevia does 10-second perioperative risk scoring (vs 2 hours for nurses) on 84,875 preoperative notes, outperforming traditional models by 38.3% AUROC. Stream does AI-powered treatment determinations in workers comp utilization review. Both require clinical trust and EMR access, both are more defensible than dashboards. But neither bears financial risk on their predictions.
**Underwriting** is where value compounds. WriteWise guarantees total pharmacy cost at a fixed PMPM, takes the risk itself — \$12.2M spread on a single employer group with sub-10% loss ratios. Sparx serves 20%+ of ACOs with \$1B+ reinsurance capacity; one contract realized 62.1% savings because the model gave ACO leadership confidence to take downside risk. Indigo prescores every US physician and auto-underwrites 20% of malpractice policies while legacy carriers price on blunt specialty + geography proxies. Carrum bundles surgical episodes, guarantees a price, and delivers 0.8% knee readmission vs 2.7% national average — the readmission delta IS the underwriting edge.

## What This Means Going Forward
The layer matters more than the vertical. A company that underwrites risk in any specialty is structurally more defensible and higher-margin than a dashboard company in a bigger market. The question for every deal: does this company bear financial risk on its predictions? If not, it's not underwriting.
The arbitrage is durable because it's a business model gap, not a technology gap. Carriers profit from opacity and population-level pricing. A platform that prices at the individual level and passes savings through is structurally misaligned with how carriers make money. UNH has 2,000+ AI engineers, Nationwide paid \$1.25B for Allstate's stop-loss business — but incumbents can't price at the right resolution because their business model depends on population pooling. Technology gaps close. Business model gaps persist.
This same stack applies wherever healthcare risk is mispriced. The segments where nobody has reached Layer 3:
**Surgical episodes** are the clearest next application. Carrum proves the bundled-episode model works for hip/knee — but the real unlock is that AI now lets you bake engagement into the underwriting model itself. A surgery bundler that automates post-op phone call follow-ups, RPM tracking, medication adherence reminders, and escalation triggers isn't just delivering care — it's producing the data that makes the underwriting model more precise. Every automated touchpoint that catches a complication early or keeps a patient compliant directly reduces the variance the model has to price for. The engagement layer becomes an input to the risk model, not a cost center bolted onto it. Briggs called this out: "patient selection (underwriting) + post-procedure compliance" are the two differentiators, and AI collapses them into one system. 29.3M adults are eligible for bariatric surgery but \<1% get it — massive latent demand suppressed by cost uncertainty that tighter underwriting could unlock. Bariatric, cardiology, and spine all moving outpatient with the same operations model: engage member → right provider → follow-up. The company that owns all three steps and underwrites the episode will capture the spread.
**Workers comp** (\$55B market, 25% admin overhead): actuarial models price on historical claim rates but miss behavioral risk factors that drive 80%+ of outcomes. Shift Health validated 35% claim reduction with continuous behavioral coaching. Nobody combines predictive risk selection with integrated intervention AND takes the underwriting risk.
**Disability** (\$142B market): return-to-work probability drops to \~50% after 6 months, below \~20% after 12. 10-15% of claims drive majority of cost. DisabilityIQ is building the temporal modeling engine — Layer 2 today, but the Layer 3 move is underwriting early intervention ROI.
**Preventative care**: the frontier Briggs flagged. No company takes actuarial risk on preventive care outcomes. If you can underwrite the claim that a preventive intervention prevents a future cost, you've created a new insurance product.
The risk is the proof cycle. AI underwriting models need 3-5 years of claims data to demonstrate sustained profitability. Reinsurers won't back unproven models — Pearl Health spent over a year finding ACO reinsurance partners. And tail risk is accelerating faster than models can be proven: gene therapy cases at one major reinsurer went from 2 to 17 in a single year, GLP-1 PMPM grew at 77% CAGR. The underwriting edge is perishable. The companies that survive the proof cycle with a closed data loop will compound advantages that are nearly impossible to replicate.
This thesis breaks if aggregation wins again at the underwriting layer — if a carrier builds or acquires equivalent AI underwriting infrastructure and prices at the same granularity while keeping the distribution advantage. That bundling cycle has happened before in healthcare and could happen again faster with AI spend accelerating consolidation.
