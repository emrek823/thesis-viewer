# Acturiarial / Underwriting Platforms

*There's an opportunity for more startups to build underwriting platforms that arbitrage imprecise risk pricing from incumbents and build defensible moats as AI commoditizes other layers of the stack.*
---
**The mispricing is real and massive.**
Healthcare risk is systematically mispriced — and not by accident. PBMs make money on spread pricing: the gap between what they charge the plan sponsor and what they pay the pharmacy. Carriers charge high-risk prices to low-risk groups and pocket the cross-subsidy. Health plans set capitation using broad demographic adjusters. In every case, pricing precisely would reveal the spread and eliminate it. Incumbents are rationally choosing to be imprecise.
The resulting gaps are enormous. WriteWise found a \$12.2M gap on a single employer group between what the incumbent PBM charged and the true cost. One Sparx contract realized 62.1% savings for an ACO. UNH, CVS, and Centene collectively mispriced \~\$19B in medical costs in a single year. 
Three structural blockers keep incumbents from closing this gap:
1. **Opacity is the margin.** Pricing precisely would destroy their own economics. They won't do it.
2. **Distribution is built around opacity.** Brokers earn commissions as a percentage of premium. Transparent, lower pricing means lower commissions. The entire channel is incentivized to maintain the current structure.
3. **Capital and compliance assume population pooling.** Reserve calculations, regulatory filings (community rating, MLR floors), and reinsurance treaties are built around group-level risk. Unwinding that is a multi-year, multi-billion dollar project no board will approve when the current model still works.
---
**Startups can exploit this by replacing blunt prices with precise ones.**
The old model across healthcare cost categories is the same: use back-end optimization tools — step edits, care management, utilization review, prior auth — to shave costs around the edges of a blunt incumbent price. The new model replaces the price entirely. Guarantee a lower number upfront, bear the risk, capture the spread.

**Layer 1 — Dashboards/Analytics.** Dashboards to upload claims, show trend, run cost analyses. Table stakes — any team can build it, no moat. But it's the GTM wedge that gets you in the door.
**Layer 2 — Workflow Integration.** Step edits, formulary design, prior auth, patient steerage. This is where you touch the operation and earn the data. Valuable, but increasingly commoditized by AI.
**Layer 3 — Underwriting.** You guarantee a number — a fixed PMPM, a shared savings rate, a bundled episode price — and you bear the risk. This is where the margin lives and where defensibility compounds.

In our portfolio, [WriteWise](https://www.writewise.com/) reprices pharmacy claims at the individual drug level and guarantees employers a fixed PMPM — they assembled 100M claims across 20 PBMs, data that doesn't exist anywhere else because PBMs won't share with each other. Outside the portfolio, [Sparx](https://usesparx.com/) underwrites ACO risk and has unlocked \$1B+ in reinsurance capacity by giving ACOs the actuarial confidence to take downside contracts. [Indigo](https://www.igohealthcare.com/) auto-underwrites 20% of malpractice submissions by prescoring every US physician individually — vs. legacy carriers pricing all OBs in Florida at \~\$120K/year regardless of history.
The edge at these companies isn't an AI breakthrough. It's clever actuarial work, data aggregation earned through GTM focus, and doing things incumbents are structurally unwilling to do.
---
**The moats compound.**
What makes this a compounding advantage rather than a one-time arbitrage is two interlocking loops:
**Data:** Every claim and outcome feeds the pricing model. WriteWise gets more precise with every employer group, Sparx improves with every ACO performance year. No new entrant can replicate years of claims history on day one.
**Capacity:** Operational execution produces the outcomes the model predicted, which validates the model, which earns reinsurance capacity, which lets you take on more risk. WriteWise runs 5% loss ratios against an industry standard of 20-30%. That track record unlocks the next tranche of capacity. A new entrant needs 3-5 years of proof cycles before reinsurers trust their model.
Critically, the operational execution IS the underwriting edge. You can't just price risk — you have to deliver on the guarantee. Carrum avoids 23% of unnecessary surgeries and hits 0.8% readmission vs. 2.7% national average. WriteWise routes patients to lower-net-cost drugs (biosimilar at \$400 vs. Stellara at \$35K). The clinical operation reduces variance, which tightens the guarantee, which earns more capacity. Companies that take risk but can't execute operationally blow up on loss ratios.
There are also creative structures that make these businesses venture-scale rather than balance-sheet-heavy. WriteWise operates as an asset-light MGU — doesn't hold risk on its balance sheet, runs 76% gross margins. Sparx unlocks capacity through surety bonds rather than letters of credit. Neither requires the capital base of a carrier.
---
**AI makes the underwriting layer more valuable over time.**
AI is compressing the value of Layers 1 and 2. Dashboards and workflow tools are getting cheaper to build and easier to replicate. That makes Layer 3 — where you guarantee a number and bear the risk — more valuable by contrast. It's the layer that can't be commoditized because it requires proprietary data, actuarial precision, and the willingness to put your own capital behind the math.
---
**The timing is now.**
Healthcare costs have gotten so extreme and volatile that back-end optimization can't keep up. GLP-1 PMPM grew 77% CAGR from \$1.43 (2019) to \$24.59 (2024). Million-dollar claims jumped 74% between 2021–2024. Gene therapy cases at one major reinsurer went from 2 to 17 in a single year. MA MLR hit 91% on \$591B in CMS payments. When trend moves this fast, incumbent models that price on 12-18 month lagging data fall further behind every quarter.
At the same time, risk is migrating to more parties across the system simultaneously. CMS is pushing ACOs toward downside risk. Employers are moving self-funded (63% of workforce). Health plans are eating losses on MA. Provider groups and MSOs want capitation but have zero actuarial talent. Reinsurers are watching their own loss ratios deteriorate. Every entity that bears risk needs what these platforms provide — and the demand is coming from every direction at once.
