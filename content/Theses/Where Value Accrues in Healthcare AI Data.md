# Where Value Accrues in Healthcare AI Data

Everyone in AI right now wants to build a context graph, talk about decision traces, or sell data to the labs. Watching our portfolio companies at both the vertical application layer and the horizontal infrastructure layer, we have seen what actually compounds — and it is not access to data. We founded Virtue on the belief that domain-specific expertise is essential to building healthcare companies, for founders and for investors. Healthcare's traditional moat has been relationships and incentive navigation: who you know at the payer, can you sell into the health system, can you navigate the structural mess of payer-provider economics. That expertise is still necessary, but it is no longer sufficient. The new moat is surgical data expertise — knowing what shape clinical, operational, and financial data needs to be in for AI to actually use it.
This pays off in both directions. If you are improving your own AI application for operational customers, the surgical work makes the model fit-for-purpose against denial avoidance, coding accuracy, prior-auth approval, or any operational outcome the customer pays for. If you are selling refined data to frontier labs, the same surgical work makes the data fit-for-purpose against a specific researcher's training requirement. Either way, the expertise is the moat. Horizontal data labelers and traditional aggregators cannot fake this, because it is earned inside healthcare workflows rather than imported into them. Imaging and wet-lab biology are the live exceptions where data-as-product genuinely works at scale, but they are narrow and rest on structurally distinctive modalities that cannot be aggregated externally.
## Why now: model improvement is migrating to real-world workflow data, and healthcare is in the wave
Post-training has matured. Frontier model improvement is no longer coming from more Q&A pairs — it is coming from real-world workflow data and the work done on top of it to produce useful training signal. Annotations on top of deployed models, and from-scratch synthetic environments, both played their role in earlier phases. The dollar is migrating to enrichment and transformation of raw real-world data, where the work on top — task extraction, grader creation, PII removal — is what makes the dataset actually useful.
In horizontal AI, all three categories of data production have real standalone vendor companies. In healthcare, the commercial action has concentrated almost entirely in the third — real-world workflow data. Annotation on deployed models lives inside application-layer companies as a feature, not a company; synthetic environments live in academia (Stanford's [HealthAdminBench](https://som-shahlab.github.io/health-admin-bench-website/), [MedHELM](https://crfm.stanford.edu/helm/medhelm/), MedAgentBench); real-world workflow data is where healthcare AI companies get built. The pattern is now too consistent to be coincidence — every serious application-layer AI company we see is training its own model against its own interaction data and getting off frontier APIs. The frontier labs are buying healthcare data as fast as they can find quality vendors; the binding constraint on the buyer side is not budget but supply of vendors with the surgical expertise to produce useful post-training data.
## Within bucket 3, two archetypes hold the surgical expertise
Real-world workflow data in healthcare is not one thing. Two distinct archetypes of application-layer company hold the surgical expertise today, distinguished by the shape of data they get from the workflow.
The first archetype owns the system of record. [Canvas Medical](https://www.canvasmedical.com/) built an open-API specialty EMR. Every encounter produces a note, every prescription touches their system, every billing action runs through their API. The data is comprehensive and longitudinal — multi-modal records that span a patient over time, with structured corrections and plugin-mediated edits attached. The surgical expertise that emerges is about extracting fit-for-AI signal from a comprehensive longitudinal corpus. Canvas knows what makes a plugin-mediated note correction useful as RL signal, what context the model needs at the point of care, and what longitudinal patterns matter. Other examples include athenahealth (multi-specialty), specialty point-of-care surfaces like Alma and Headway in behavioral health, and AI-native EMRs in fragmented specialty categories.
The second archetype deploys inside someone else's surface. Michael Wornow's [Kinetic Systems](https://www.kineticsystems.ai/), built on top of HealthAdminBench, sells computer-use agents into payers, providers, and RCM vendors that run prior auth, DME, and appeals workflows. The data is not comprehensive — it is the slice of the workflow the agent touches. But that slice includes something the first archetype does not have: the customer's specific business rules, payer-by-payer adjudication patterns, edge cases, and structured operational ontology that the team extracts to make the agent perform. The surgical expertise is structured rule extraction; the customer-specific ontology compounds across deployments.

The structural reason both archetypes win is verification. AI improves on tasks in proportion to how verifiable those tasks are. Healthcare's open-ended problems score badly on verifiability — clinicians disagree on the right answer for any non-trivial case, ground truth is slow, expensive, and noisy. But the specific tasks bucket-3 application companies operate on score extremely well: a denial is avoided or it isn't, a code is accepted or it isn't, a prior auth is approved or it isn't. The operational customer paying for the outcome is the verification mechanism. Horizontal labelers can build datasets, but they cannot build verification — that lives in the workflow, and you cannot replicate it from outside.
## Underneath the application layer, the dollar is migrating from access to activation
Horizontal data infrastructure in healthcare has historically been access-focused. [Datavant](https://www.datavant.com) aggregates and links claims across 70+ data sources. [Truveta](https://www.truveta.com/) corrals clinical data from 30+ member health systems into a 130M-patient longitudinal corpus and trains a foundation model on top. [Komodo Health](https://www.komodohealth.com) aggregates 325M-patient claims and sells analytics at a \$3.3B valuation. IQVIA built the legacy giant of pharma real-world data aggregation. The dominant model has been: build the network, accumulate the data, sell access to pharma and increasingly to AI labs.
That model is now being disrupted by a different focus — not access, but activation. Frontier labs do not buy data the way pharma does. Pharma buys broad real-world data for retrospective analytics; labs demand surgical refinement to a specific researcher's training requirement, delivered continuously, with verification built in. Even application-layer companies with native data access frequently cannot use their data for training because of contractual or privacy walls between the deployed product and the training pipeline. That gap is what activation infrastructure fills.
[Integral](https://www.integral.io) is our portfolio bet on this layer. Their entity-preserving methods preserve longitudinal patient continuity while creating defensible re-identification posture. The rail makes a system-of-record incumbent's exhaust monetizable to labs without ceding compliance position; it makes an application-layer agent's structured operational data refineable for partner labs; and it extends from healthcare into financial and consumer regulated data because the activation work is the same fundamental problem regardless of which application-layer company sits on top. Access at this layer is becoming table stakes. Persistent, compliant, continuous activation is where the moat actually lives — and the activation layer is structurally underweighted by capital today.
## So what? 
The surgical-expertise test applies differently by category. For system-of-record companies, the question is whether the workflow itself genuinely sticks and whether the team can extract fit-for-AI signal from their longitudinal corpus. For application-layer companies, the question is whether the customer-specific operational knowledge captured per deployment generalizes into a reusable platform, or whether every deal is fully bespoke services. For horizontal data infrastructure, the question is whether the company is migrating with the dollar from access to activation, and whether their layer compresses regulatory drag for the application companies above it without competing with them.
We pass on pitches that have data access without surgical activation expertise. "We have unique data." "We will be the healthcare labeler." "We will be the data marketplace." All three bet on frontier labs as the dominant buyer, but labs structurally cannot pay marketplace-clearing prices: they intentionally avoid single-vendor concentration to prevent another NVIDIA situation, and operational buyers pay 10-100x more for the same intelligence delivered as outcome. The exception is structurally distinctive modalities. Imaging is the live example (RadNet, Aidoc economics), and the frontier labs are now actively acquiring wet-lab and bio-data assets at scale, including small wet labs and data networks. Outside that narrow window, data access without surgical activation expertise does not compound.
The reason the frontier labs themselves cannot solve this is organizational. Labs are built to serve one model to many customers; specialization requires the inverse, and fine-tuning is a sideline at the labs because it has to be a sideline. Horizontal labelers face the same structural problem in healthcare: hiring physicians does not generate the workflow-based feedback loops that the application-layer companies have, and without those loops the data the labelers produce is generic, decontextualized, and increasingly commoditized. Vendors selling data who do not understand how model improvement actually happens will not last.
We'd love to chat with founders building application-layer healthcare AI companies with deep surgical data expertise, distinctive-modality data plays in imaging or biology, or activation infrastructure underneath the vertical thesis.
