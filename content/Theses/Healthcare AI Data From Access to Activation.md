# Healthcare AI Data: From Access to Activation

We’ve been working portfolio companies and  our portfolio companies at the vertical application layer and the horizontal infrastructure layer, we've seen what actually compounds — and it is not access to data. We founded Virtue on the belief that domain-specific expertise is essential to building healthcare companies, for founders and for investors. The kind of expertise that creates defensibility has shifted. Healthcare AI data is moving toward real-world workflow data — the exhaust of deployed applications, plus the work done on top of it. Within that, access is becoming table stakes; activation is where the moat lives.
## Real-world workflow data is where healthcare AI data is moving
[Sid Potdar's framework](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/data-labeling-other-euphemisms-sid-potdar-txdsc/) splits post-training data into three categories: annotations on live model output, from-scratch synthetic environments, and real-world workflow data (with the transformation work done on top). In horizontal AI, all three have real standalone vendor companies. In healthcare, the commercial action has concentrated almost entirely in the third — and the work on top (task extraction, grader creation, PII removal) is what makes that data actually useful for AI.

The first category lives inside application-layer companies as a feature, not a company. The second lives in academia mostly. 
The third is where healthcare AI companies get built — and the surgical expertise to make that data useful is held by two distinct archetypes:
- **System of record** ([Canvas Medical](https://www.canvasmedical.com/)). Owns the surface. Every encounter, prescription, and billing action flows through them by default. The data is comprehensive and longitudinal; the surgical expertise is about extracting fit-for-AI signal from that corpus.
- **Application-layer embedded** (Michael Wornow's [Kinetic Systems](https://www.kineticsystems.ai/)). Deploys inside someone else's workflow. The data is a slice — but the slice includes the customer's specific business rules, payer-by-payer adjudication patterns, and structured operational ontology. The surgical expertise is structured rule extraction.
Both archetypes have surgical data expertise earned inside workflows that horizontal labelers cannot replicate. Both compound differently, and they don't substitute for each other.
## Access is becoming table stakes; activation is where the moat lives
Horizontal data infrastructure in healthcare has historically been access-focused. [Datavant](https://www.datavant.com) aggregates and links claims across 70+ data sources. [Truveta](https://www.truveta.com/) corrals clinical data from 30+ member health systems into a 130M-patient longitudinal corpus. [Komodo Health](https://www.komodohealth.com) aggregates 325M-patient claims at a \$3.3B valuation. IQVIA built the legacy giant. The dominant model: build the network, accumulate data, sell access.
That model is being disrupted. Frontier labs don't buy data the way pharma does. Pharma buys broad real-world data for retrospective analytics; labs demand surgical refinement to specific researcher requirements, delivered continuously, with verification built in. Even application-layer companies with native data access frequently can't use it for training because of contractual or privacy walls between the deployed product and the training pipeline. That gap is what activation infrastructure fills. [Shubh Sinha at Integral has been making this point](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/integral-data-privacy-programs-ai-shubh-sinha-dl5ce/) clearly — access is table stakes; activation is the moat.
[Integral](https://www.integral.io) is our portfolio bet on this layer. Their entity-preserving methods preserve longitudinal patient continuity while creating defensible re-identification posture. The rail makes a system-of-record incumbent's exhaust monetizable to labs without ceding compliance position; it makes an application-layer agent's structured operational data refineable for partner labs; and it extends from healthcare into financial and consumer regulated data because the activation work is the same fundamental problem regardless of which application sits on top.
## What we see going forward
**1. Every serious app-layer AI company will train its own model.** They're moving off frontier APIs onto specialized models trained against their own interaction data. Healthcare is squarely in this wave — Abridge, OpenEvidence, Hippocratic, and others have already moved. The reward signal for being good at any specific workflow lives inside that workflow's product, and nowhere else.
**2. Standalone data assets become less valuable.** Outside structurally distinctive modalities — imaging and some antibody/wet-lab bio data — pure data plays without activation work don't compound. Frontier labs are picky buyers, and operational outcomes pay multiples more.
**3. The activation layer becomes its own investable category.** Compliance, sanitization, and surgical refinement for AI training requirements stop being a feature of data aggregators and become a standalone horizontal. We've made our portfolio bet with Integral; expect more entrants over the next 24 months.
We'd love to chat with founders building application-layer healthcare AI companies, distinctive-modality data plays in imaging or biology, or activation infrastructure underneath the vertical thesis.
