## Overview

Specialty pharmacy is now the largest PBM profit pool at $10.8B (37% of total), up from $2.4B (12%) in 2012. Specialty drug revenues will exceed $50B in 2026 and could reach $965B by 2030 at 20% CAGR. This concentration happened because every other PBM profit source got squeezed: rebates collapsed from 47% to 7% retention, spread compressed, and admin/GPO fees ballooned to compensate. PBM-affiliated specialty pharmacies (CVS, UNH, Cigna) control 70% of specialty dispensing, generating $200B+ in revenues through bundled rebate guarantees and limited distribution networks.

The opportunity is in the operational layer between specialty pharmacy's massive economics and the buyers trying to capture value from it. Health systems are building specialty pharmacies as their sole growth engine (26%+ now own one, up from 8% in 2015) but lack tools to extract margin. 9 in 10 large hospitals have in-house specialty pharmacy; 70% are exclusive pharmacy for self-insured employee plans. Specialty drugs hit $568B in 2026, with 9 of 10 top-selling drugs by revenue but only 2% of prescriptions. Every script a health system captures is one less flowing through Accredo, CVS Specialty, or OptumRx. The buyers are fragmented (CPOs, benefits directors, pharmacy operators) but the operational gaps are consistent: PA automation, cost analytics, and margin intelligence.

## Bull Case

**PA automation is proven with extreme lock-in.** Latent Health is deployed at 25+ health systems (Yale, Ochsner, Northwestern, MetroHealth), raised $50M Series B. Ochsner cut PA times by 75% using Latent. CPO at Cone Health: "Would need an act of God to switch" once implemented. Northwestern planning to phase out CoverMyMeds for Latent. IT bandwidth and AI governance boards create 1+ year implementation cycles, making switching costs prohibitive.

**CPO willingness to pay for analytics is $400-500K.** No vendor does specialty pharmacy leakage analytics well today. Real-time margin dashboards by physician, drug, payer, and clinic are greenfield. The business case for every other investment starts with understanding the opportunity.

**Specialty pharmacy is the sole growth engine for health systems.** Health system margins are under pressure from every direction. Specialty pharmacy generates 2-3x infusion revenue. McKinsey projects ambulatory infusion and hospital specialty pharmacy to grow >10% annually through 2029.

**Market is wide open despite early movers.** Latent has ~12 partners showing publicly. Mandolin has 700+ clinics but no health systems. Plenful maybe two. CPO says market "has not been captured at all." A strong entrant with differentiated approach can still win.

---

## Bear Case

**Epic commoditizes PA automation.** Epic is developing AI-powered PA automation and generative AI for clinical documentation. Epic's UGM 2025 showcased 100+ AI features. Northwestern IT pharmacist: Epic is "behind" today but Epic has distribution to 250+ health systems and a track record of eventually absorbing point solutions.

**CPO psychology resists adoption.** Chief pharmacy officers have built empires measured by FTE count and revenue growth. Selling automation that reduces headcount threatens the buyer's status. This slows adoption even when ROI is clear.

**AI governance creates year-long implementation cycles.** Health systems have created AI governance structures "filled with people that have no idea what the actual workflows are." Vendors couldn't even get fair evaluations because other solutions were already in motion and governance boards would take 8 months to compare. This favors first movers and penalizes late entrants.

**What would have to be true for you to be wrong?**

Two scenarios break this thesis. First, Epic ships genuinely competitive PA automation that is "free" to Epic shops and good enough that health system CPOs don't need standalone vendors. The switching cost moat evaporates because health systems never leave Epic.

Second, health systems fail to build specialty pharmacies at scale. If manufacturer 340B restrictions, capital constraints, or regulatory changes make owned specialty pharmacy economically unviable, the buyer base for these tools shrinks. Currently 26% of hospitals own specialty pharmacies; if that growth stalls, the opportunity contracts.

## Startup Opportunities

**1. Specialty Pharmacy PA Automation (Proven -- Latent leads)**
- Why this follows: PA is the highest-volume administrative burden in specialty pharmacy. Every script requires PA; delays kill revenue.
- Wedge: Epic/EHR integration, specialty drug focus.
- The edge: Deep EHR integration, 97% clinical accuracy, automated submission + follow-up. Latent proven at 25+ systems. One-day implementation capability. CPO chose Latent over Mandolin because "specialty is where all the money is."
- Buyers: Chief Pharmacy Officers, VP Pharmacy Operations.
- Risk: Epic catches up or becomes "good enough." ACV compression if bundled into EMR.

**2. Specialty Drug Cost Analytics & Margin Intelligence (Greenfield -- highest ACV)**
- Why this follows: CPOs can't make the case for investment without knowing the opportunity. Analytics precedes everything else.
- Wedge: Real-time leakage dashboard showing margin opportunity by physician, drug, payer, clinic.
- The edge: No one does this well. CPO would pay $400-500K just for analytics before PA automation. Creates business case for every other investment. Lower IT lift than PA automation.
- Buyers: Chief Pharmacy Officers, CFOs.
- Risk: Could be built by EMR vendors or existing analytics platforms. May be a feature, not a company.

**3. Pharma-Funded PA Automation (Emerging -- Tandem model)**
- Why this follows: 37% of prescriptions abandoned due to PA delays. Pharma loses $250B+ from non-adherence.
- Wedge: Free for providers, pharma pays for brand lift. EHR integration via "prescribe to Tandem" like a pharmacy.
- The edge: Different GTM (providers, not health systems), different payer (pharma, not hospitals). $40-100K revenue per physician if pharma sees 10x ROI. Tandem at 1,035 providers, $5M run rate.
- Buyers: Pharma commercial teams (patient access), specialty prescribers.
- Risk: Regulatory scrutiny on pharma funding provider tools. Tandem has first-mover advantage in neuro/derm.

**4. Specialty Pharmacy Build/Operate Services (Early -- services model)**
- Why this follows: Health systems want owned specialty pharmacy economics but lack capabilities.
- Wedge: Accreditation, compliance, launch support, ongoing management for health system specialty pharmacies.
- The edge: VytlOne/ProxsysRx model validates ($750-1000/bed/month). 26%+ of hospitals already building. Manufacturer 340B restrictions push them to owned vs. contract.
- Buyers: Health system CFOs, Pharmacy directors building new programs.
- Risk: Service-heavy, harder to scale. Competes with consulting firms (Huron, Vizient).

## Open Questions

**Will Epic's PA automation reach "good enough" by 2027?** If Epic ships competitive PA that's bundled with EHR, Latent's $400K ACV becomes hard to defend. Epic previewed new interoperability features in 2025 but remains early.
--> Would validate/invalidate: The "act of God" switching cost claim. If Epic gets good enough, standalone PA vendors face ACV compression or displacement.

**Does the specialty pharmacy analytics gap persist or get filled by incumbents?** No vendor does leakage analytics well today, but Epic, Loopback Analytics, or new entrants could build it.
--> Would validate/invalidate: The greenfield nature of the analytics opportunity. If someone ships this in 2026, first-mover matters more than we think.

**Can health systems continue building specialty pharmacies despite 340B pressure?** 39 manufacturers now restrict 340B pricing. If restrictions expand and make health system specialty pharmacy economically unviable, the buyer base for PA automation and analytics contracts.
--> Would validate/invalidate: The premise that health systems are the right buyer. If owned specialty pharmacy growth stalls, the opportunity shifts to other buyers.
