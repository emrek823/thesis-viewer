## Overview

There's existing frameworks for clinical competence and we can use this to simplify some of the commentary around different benchmarks in clinical AI. [[Sources/Research-Papers/Miller's Pyramid Assessment of Clinical Skills Competence Performance|Miller's Pyramid (1990)]] separates what a doctor *knows* from what they can *do* with four levels from knowledge recall ("Knows") to independent clinical practice ("Does"). We use this framework because it exposes exactly where LLMs hit their ceiling in clinical AI. Each rung up the ladder adds temporal depth, causal structure, and multimodal complexity.

The pattern is not that benchmarks get harder but that each rung measures a fundamentally different capability, and the data access requirement changes at each step. The wall between rungs is not transformer vs JEPA but what the model is trained to predict and what data it needs access to. Rungs 1-2 are solvable with public text and general-purpose language models. Rung 3 requires multi-turn clinical reasoning that [[Sources/Research-Papers/MedQA-CS Benchmarking LLM Clinical Skills Using AI-SCE Framework|MedQA-CS (2024)]] proved breaks even at 90+ knowledge scores, and [[Sources/Research-Papers/MedPI Evaluating AI Systems in Medical Patient-facing Interactions|MedPI]] confirmed across all 9 frontier models on 105 ACGME-aligned dimensions. Rung 4 requires structured clinical event data that lives inside health systems — [[Sources/Research-Papers/Lavender Jiang - Generalist Foundation Models Are Not Clinical Enough for Hospital Operations|Lang1]] showed generalist models (even 671x larger) score only 36.6-71.7% AUROC zero-shot on hospital operations, while clinical pretraining closes the gap by 3.64-6.75%. [[Sources/Research-Papers/EHRSHOT An EHR Benchmark for Few-Shot Evaluation of Foundation Models|EHRSHOT]] exposed this structurally: traditional ML achieves 0.847 AUROC where GPT-4 manages 0.629 on coded EHR data (though this was tested in 2023 and has not been retested with reasoning models). Rung 5 requires longitudinal patient trajectories under intervention — data that only exists behind institutional walls. [[Sources/LLM-Chats/Claude-Epic Curiosity healthcare foundation model memo|Epic's CoMET]] achieves 0.925 AUROC on CHF trajectory using a standard transformer on 115B events from 118M patients. [[Sources/Research-Papers/The Patient is not a Moving Document A World Model Training Paradigm for Longitudinal EHR|SMB-Structure]] takes a different architectural approach (JEPA+SFT hybrid) and achieves +7.2% c-index on temporal tasks across 23K oncology patients. The [[Sources/Research-Papers/NEP Framework Building the EHR Foundation Model via Next Event Prediction|NEP framework]] fine-tunes Llama-3.1 on clinical event sequences and achieves +3.5% c-index on pancreatic cancer — technically an LLM backbone with a clinical training objective. The architecture is secondary; the data access requirement is the moat. Multiple architectures can reach Rung 5, but all require institutional clinical data that doesn't exist on the open internet. JEPA-style approaches have validated across clinical modalities — [[Sources/Research-Papers/RadJEPA Radiology Encoder for Chest X-Rays via Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture|RadJEPA]] for radiology, [[Sources/Research-Papers/EchoJEPA A Latent Predictive Foundation Model for Echocardiography|EchoJEPA]] for cardiac measurement (+20% on LVEF, zero-shot pediatric surpassing fine-tuned baselines) — but they are one path to Rung 5, not the only one. A methodological caveat: both HealthBench and MedPI use LLM-as-judge evaluation, which may share systematic biases with the models under test. The [[Sources/Research-Papers/Knowledge-Practice Performance Gap in Clinical LLMs Systematic Review of 39 Benchmarks|39-benchmark systematic review]] confirms the knowledge-practice gap holds across evaluation methods, not just LLM-judged ones.

## What This Means Going Forward

The ladder reveals that clinical AI is not one market but distinct capability layers, and the key question for each layer is defensibility: where does data access create moats versus where does the latest off-the-shelf model make the previous one obsolete? Most healthcare AI investment has concentrated at Rungs 1-3: ambient scribes, chatbots, triage, documentation — [[Sources/Market-Research-PDFs/Bessemer State of Health AI 2026|55% of all health tech funding now goes to AI ($14B across 527 deals)]], predominantly at these lower rungs. At Rungs 1-2, each new model generation commoditizes the previous one — no proprietary data required, no switching costs beyond workflow integration. Companies that win at conversation will not automatically win at prediction. Rungs 4-5 require access to millions of longitudinal patient records that live inside Epic, health systems, and claims databases. The gap is structural: a [[Sources/Research-Papers/Knowledge-Practice Performance Gap in Clinical LLMs Systematic Review of 39 Benchmarks|systematic review of 39 benchmarks]] shows the 39-45 point knowledge-practice drop is consistent across model families, languages, and specialties — this is not a "GPT-5 will fix it" problem. Reasoning models (o3, chain-of-thought) are compressing the gap at Rungs 2-3 (o3 doubled GPT-4o on HealthBench in months), which means the window for building durable value at those rungs is closing.

The data moat at Rung 5 is already materializing. [[Sources/LLM-Chats/Claude-Epic Curiosity healthcare foundation model memo|Epic's CoMET]] achieves 0.925 AUROC on chronic heart failure trajectory prediction using 115B events from 118M patients across 1,760+ hospitals — a dataset no startup can replicate. If prediction is primarily a data problem, Epic's [305M+ patient moat and one-click distribution across 42% of US acute care hospitals](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/epics-dominance-in-12-numbers/) is the advantage. But the architecture reaching Rung 5 matters less than the data access: the [[Sources/Research-Papers/NEP Framework Building the EHR Foundation Model via Next Event Prediction|NEP framework]] achieves trajectory prediction with Llama-3.1, [[Sources/Research-Papers/The Patient is not a Moving Document A World Model Training Paradigm for Longitudinal EHR|SMB-Structure]] uses JEPA+SFT, and CoMET uses a domain-specific transformer. Multiple architectures work. None work without institutional clinical data. The startups that know which rung they are building for — and have the data access required for that rung — will build defensible companies. The ones pitching "AI for healthcare" without specifying the rung or the data source are building on a commodity.

The real question for this framework is not whether one model conquers all rungs — it's where data access creates defensibility over time versus where the latest model is sufficient. At Rungs 1-2, no proprietary data is required and each model generation resets the competitive landscape. At Rungs 4-5, you need access to longitudinal patient records behind institutional walls. The framework breaks if frontier models achieve Rung 5 performance without institutional clinical data — if synthetic data or transfer from text to structured prediction closes the gap. Current evidence says it hasn't.

## The Benchmark Staleness Problem

Most numbers in this ladder are already dated. EHRSHOT was published in 2023 and tested GPT-4 — no one has retested with o3, GPT-5, or reasoning models on structured EHR prediction. HealthBench (2025) and MedPI (2026) are more current but still tested only the generation of frontier models available at publication. The [[Sources/Research-Papers/ARISE State of Clinical AI Report 2026|ARISE State of Clinical AI Report (2026)]], the first annual Stanford-Harvard synthesis of the field, found that nearly half of 500+ medical AI studies still use exam-style questions and only 5% use real patient data. Even top-performing models make 12-15 severe errors per 100 clinical cases. The report's core finding: "With the saturation of traditional QA benchmark scores, there is a need for benchmarking on multi-turn unstructured real-world data."

**There is no [[Sources/Research-Papers/METR Model Evaluation and Threat Research|METR]] for healthcare.** METR (Model Evaluation & Threat Research) is the independent nonprofit that systematically evaluates frontier AI capabilities for safety — partnering with Anthropic and OpenAI for pre-deployment assessments, running standardized task suites (RE-Bench, HCAST), and tracking that AI task-completion length doubles every ~7 months. They are funded independently (Audacious Project, foundations) so they can maintain credibility. Healthcare has no equivalent today. 

How can we build the METR for healthcare? Who should build this?
