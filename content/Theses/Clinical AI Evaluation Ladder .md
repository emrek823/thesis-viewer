
## Overview

There's existing frameworks for clinical competence and we can use this to simplify some of the commentary around different benchmarks in clinical AI. [[Sources/Research-Papers/Miller's Pyramid Assessment of Clinical Skills Competence Performance|Miller's Pyramid (1990)]] separates what a doctor *knows* from what they can *do* with four levels from knowledge recall ("Knows") to independent clinical practice ("Does"). We use this framework because it exposes exactly where LLMs hit their ceiling in clinical AI. LLMs score 84-90% on knowledge-based assessments but drop to 45-69% on practice-based tasks and 40-50% on safety-critical decisions. Each rung up the ladder adds temporal depth, causal structure, and multimodal complexity.

| Rung | Benchmark | What It Actually Measures | Miller Level | LLM Performance | Capability Signal |
| ---- | --------- | ------------------------- | ------------ | --------------- | ----------------- |
| 1    | **USMLE** | Can you pass a medical exam? Factual recall from textbooks — "what drug treats X," "what's the mechanism of Y." No patient, no time, no ambiguity. | Knows | 100% (saturated) | Pattern matching on training data. Solved. |
| 2    | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/HealthBench Evaluating Large Language Models Towards Improved Human Health\|HealthBench]]** (OpenAI, 2025) | Can you have a useful conversation with a patient? Single-turn: patient describes symptoms, model responds. Tests whether the response is accurate, safe, and helpful — judged by 262 physicians across 48K rubric criteria. | Knows How | o3: 60%, GPT-4o: 32% | Improving fast (+28% in months), but 40% gap to ceiling |
| 3    | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/MedPI Evaluating AI Systems in Medical Patient-facing Interactions\|MedPI]]** (Lumos, 2026) | Can you conduct a full clinical encounter? Multi-turn: model must gather history, ask follow-ups, build a differential diagnosis, and recommend treatment — across 7K simulated patient-doctor conversations scored on 105 ACGME-aligned dimensions. | Shows How | All 9 frontier LLMs fail on differential diagnosis; Claude ≤15% on completeness | Maintaining a coherent patient model across turns breaks token prediction |
| 3.5  | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/NYUTron Health System-Scale Language Models Are All-Purpose Prediction Engines\|NYUTron]]** (Jiang et al., Nature 2023) | Can you predict what happens to this patient from their chart? Reads unstructured clinical notes (4.1B words, 387K patients) and predicts static outcomes: readmission, mortality, length of stay. First clinical LM deployed live in a hospital. | Shows How → Does (text) | Readmission: 0.799 AUROC (+5.4%); Mortality: 0.949 (+7.4%) | Clinical LMs beat general LLMs on prediction — but still text-based, still static outcomes, still "will they?" not "how will they?" |
| 4    | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/EHRSHOT An EHR Benchmark for Few-Shot Evaluation of Foundation Models\|EHRSHOT]]** (Stanford, 2023) | Can you predict outcomes from structured medical records? Not notes — coded data: diagnosis codes, procedures, lab values, medications across 41.6M clinical events for 6.7K patients. 15 prediction tasks including readmission, ICU transfer, disease onset. | Shows How → Does (structured) | Traditional ML: 0.847 vs GPT-4: 0.629. LLM gains compress to <1% | Structured temporal data is fundamentally non-linguistic. Text-trained models hit a wall here. |
| 5    | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/The Patient is not a Moving Document A World Model Training Paradigm for Longitudinal EHR\|JEPA c-index]]** (SMB @ MSK, 2026) | Can you predict how a patient's disease will evolve under a specific treatment? Not "will they die" but "given this patient's state today + Drug X, what is their state in 6 months?" Dynamic trajectory prediction under intervention, across 23K oncology patients and 323K+ patient-years. | Does | +7.2% c-index on temporal tasks. Treatment change: 0.78 vs 0.70 (+18%). Sarcoma: +8.5% | Predicting biological futures, not plausible text. Clinical training objectives exceed text-prediction ceiling. |

The pattern is not that benchmarks get harder but that each rung measures a fundamentally different capability and the training objective required to solve it changes. Rungs 1-2 are linguistic tasks where token prediction works well and is improving fast. [[Sources/Research-Papers/MedQA-CS Benchmarking LLM Clinical Skills Using AI-SCE Framework|MedQA-CS (2024)]] proved the break explicitly: LLMs scoring 90+ on knowledge benchmarks dropped sharply on clinical skills like differential diagnosis. [[Sources/Research-Papers/MedPI Evaluating AI Systems in Medical Patient-facing Interactions|MedPI]] extended this across 105 ACGME-aligned dimensions and saw that frontier models fail on differential diagnosis. [[Sources/Research-Papers/NYUTron Health System-Scale Language Models Are All-Purpose Prediction Engines|NYUTron]] showed clinical language models trained on institutional notes outperform general LLMs by 5-15% on prediction, and [[Sources/Research-Papers/Lavender Jiang - Generalist Foundation Models Are Not Clinical Enough for Hospital Operations|Lavender Jiang's ongoing work]] finds that "generalist foundation models are not clinical enough for hospital operations". NYUTron only predicts static outcomes from text. [[Sources/Research-Papers/EHRSHOT An EHR Benchmark for Few-Shot Evaluation of Foundation Models|EHRSHOT]] hits the real wall: structured EHR data is fundamentally non-linguistic, and traditional ML achieves 0.847 AUROC where GPT-4 manages 0.629. At the top of the ladder, the question changes entirely: a clinician asks "given this patient + Drug X, what happens in 6 months?". JEPA-style architectures have validated across clinical modalities — [[Sources/Research-Papers/RadJEPA Radiology Encoder for Chest X-Rays via Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture|RadJEPA]] for radiology image encoding, [[Sources/Research-Papers/EchoJEPA A Latent Predictive Foundation Model for Echocardiography|EchoJEPA]] for cardiac measurement (+20% on LVEF, zero-shot pediatric surpassing fine-tuned baselines) — but few have demonstrated Rung 5 trajectory prediction under intervention. [[Sources/Research-Papers/The Patient is not a Moving Document A World Model Training Paradigm for Longitudinal EHR|Standard Model Bio]] is the furthest along.

## What This Means Going Forward

The ladder reveals that clinical AI is not one market but distinct capability layers, each with different training objectives, buyers, and competitive dynamics. Most healthcare AI investment has concentrated at Rungs 1-3: ambient scribes, chatbots, triage, documentation. All LLM-native, all improving, all commoditizing as frontier models get better at conversation. Companies that win at conversation will not automatically win at prediction. Rungs 4-5 require different training objectives (next-event prediction on structured clinical data, trajectory simulation) and different data (longitudinal structured records + imaging + genomics, not just clinical text), and different buyers (pharma paying for trajectory prediction in trial design and treatment selection, not health systems paying per-note for documentation). The gap is structural: a [[Sources/Research-Papers/Knowledge-Practice Performance Gap in Clinical LLMs Systematic Review of 39 Benchmarks|systematic review of 39 benchmarks]] shows the 39-45 point knowledge-practice drop is consistent across model families, languages, and specialties — this is not a "GPT-5 will fix it" problem. Reasoning models (o3, chain-of-thought) may compress the gap at Rungs 2-3, but the EHRSHOT numbers at Rung 4 (traditional ML 0.847 vs GPT-4 0.629) have not moved.

The evolving dynamics here are where data matters more than training objective. [[Sources/LLM-Chats/Claude-Epic Curiosity healthcare foundation model memo|Epic's Curiosity]] achieves 0.925 AUROC on chronic heart failure trajectory using standard transformers on 115B events — no JEPA required. If prediction is primarily a data problem, Epic's 300M patient moat and one-click distribution to 1,760+ hospitals have the advantage. There is also a blurriness at the boundary where the [[Sources/Research-Papers/NEP Framework Building the EHR Foundation Model via Next Event Prediction|NEP framework]] fine-tunes Llama-3.1 on clinical event sequences and achieves +3.5% c-index on pancreatic cancer. It's technically still an LLM backbone but with a clinical training objective — the wall is what the model is trained to predict (next token on text vs next event on structured clinical data), not whether the backbone is a transformer or JEPA. The startups that know which rung they are building for, and why text-trained models structurally cannot serve that rung, will build defensible companies. The ones pitching "AI for healthcare" without specifying the rung are building on a commodity.

This framework breaks if a unified foundation model achieves >90% performance across every rung — from USMLE through trajectory prediction — collapsing the layers into "scale solves everything." Current evidence says this hasn't happened, but reasoning model improvements at Rungs 2-3 (o3 doubling GPT-4o on HealthBench) mean the lower rungs are already compressing.

## The Benchmark Staleness Problem

Most numbers in this ladder are already dated. EHRSHOT was published in 2023 and tested GPT-4 — no one has retested with o3, GPT-5, or reasoning models on structured EHR prediction. HealthBench (2025) and MedPI (2026) are more current but still tested only the generation of frontier models available at publication. The [[Sources/Research-Papers/ARISE State of Clinical AI Report 2026|ARISE State of Clinical AI Report (2026)]], the first annual Stanford-Harvard synthesis of the field, found that nearly half of 500+ medical AI studies still use exam-style questions and only 5% use real patient data. Even top-performing models make 12-15 severe errors per 100 clinical cases. The report's core finding: "With the saturation of traditional QA benchmark scores, there is a need for benchmarking on multi-turn unstructured real-world data."

**There is no [[Sources/Research-Papers/METR Model Evaluation and Threat Research|METR]] for healthcare.** METR (Model Evaluation & Threat Research) is the independent nonprofit that systematically evaluates frontier AI capabilities for safety — partnering with Anthropic and OpenAI for pre-deployment assessments, running standardized task suites (RE-Bench, HCAST), and tracking that AI task-completion length doubles every ~7 months. They are funded independently (Audacious Project, foundations) so they can maintain credibility. Healthcare has no equivalent today. 

How can we change this?
