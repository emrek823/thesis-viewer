## Overview

Medical education has a framework for assessing clinical competence and we can use this to simplify somewhat all the commentary around different benchmarks in clinical medicine. [[Sources/Research-Papers/Miller's Pyramid Assessment of Clinical Skills Competence Performance|Miller's Pyramid (1990)]] separates what a doctor *knows* from what they can *do* — four levels from knowledge recall ("Knows") to independent clinical practice ("Does"). We use this framework because it exposes exactly where LLMs hit their ceiling in clinical AI. LLMs score 84-90% on knowledge-based assessments but drop to 45-69% on practice-based tasks and 40-50% on safety-critical decisions. Each rung up the ladder adds temporal depth, causal structure, and multimodal complexity.

| Rung | Benchmark | What It Actually Measures | Miller Level | LLM Performance | Architectural Signal |
|------|-----------|--------------------------|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| 1 | **USMLE** | Can you pass a medical exam? Factual recall from textbooks — "what drug treats X," "what's the mechanism of Y." No patient, no time, no ambiguity. | Knows | 100% (saturated) | Pattern matching on training data. Solved. |
| 2 | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/HealthBench Evaluating Large Language Models Towards Improved Human Health|HealthBench]]** (OpenAI, 2025) | Can you have a useful conversation with a patient? Single-turn: patient describes symptoms, model responds. Tests whether the response is accurate, safe, and helpful — judged by 262 physicians across 48K rubric criteria. | Knows How | o3: 60%, GPT-4o: 32% | Improving fast (+28% in months), but 40% gap to ceiling |
| 3 | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/MedPI Evaluating AI Systems in Medical Patient-facing Interactions|MedPI]]** (Lumos, 2026) | Can you conduct a full clinical encounter? Multi-turn: model must gather history, ask follow-ups, build a differential diagnosis, and recommend treatment — across 7K simulated patient-doctor conversations scored on 105 ACGME-aligned dimensions. | Shows How | All 9 frontier LLMs fail on differential diagnosis; Claude ≤15% on completeness | Maintaining a coherent patient model across turns breaks token prediction |
| 3.5 | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/NYUTron Health System-Scale Language Models Are All-Purpose Prediction Engines|NYUTron]]** (Jiang et al., Nature 2023) | Can you predict what happens to this patient from their chart? Reads unstructured clinical notes (4.1B words, 387K patients) and predicts static outcomes: readmission, mortality, length of stay. First clinical LM deployed live in a hospital. | Shows How → Does (text) | Readmission: 0.799 AUROC (+5.4%); Mortality: 0.949 (+7.4%) | Clinical LMs beat general LLMs on prediction — but still text-based, still static outcomes, still "will they?" not "how will they?" |
| 4 | **[[Sources/Research-Papers/EHRSHOT An EHR Benchmark for Few-Shot Evaluation of Foundation Models|EHRSHOT]]** (Stanford, 2023) | Can you predict outcomes from structured medical records? Not notes — coded data: diagnosis codes, procedures, lab values, medications across 41.6M clinical events for 6.7K patients. 15 prediction tasks including readmission, ICU transfer, disease onset. | Shows How → Does (structured) | Traditional ML: 0.847 vs GPT-4: 0.629. LLM gains compress to <1% | Structured temporal data is fundamentally non-linguistic. Architectural wall. |
| 5 | **JEPA c-index** (SMB @ MSK, 2026) | Can you predict how a patient's disease will evolve under a specific treatment? Not "will they die" but "given this patient's state today + Drug X, what is their state in 6 months?" Dynamic trajectory prediction under intervention, across 23K oncology patients and 323K+ patient-years. | Does | +7.2% c-index on temporal tasks. Treatment change: 0.78 vs 0.70 (+18%). Sarcoma: +8.5% | Predicting biological futures, not plausible text. World models exceed LLM ceiling. |

The pattern is not that benchmarks get harder — each rung measures a fundamentally different capability and the architecture required to solve it changes. Rungs 1-2 are linguistic tasks where token prediction works well and is improving fast. [[Sources/Research-Papers/MedQA-CS Benchmarking LLM Clinical Skills Using AI-SCE Framework|MedQA-CS (2024)]] proved the break explicitly: LLMs scoring 90+ on knowledge benchmarks dropped sharply on clinical skills like differential diagnosis. [[Sources/Research-Papers/MedPI Evaluating AI Systems in Medical Patient-facing Interactions|MedPI]] extended this across 105 ACGME-aligned dimensions — every frontier model fails on differential dx. [[Sources/Research-Papers/NYUTron Health System-Scale Language Models Are All-Purpose Prediction Engines|NYUTron]] showed clinical language models trained on institutional notes outperform general LLMs by 5-15% on prediction, and [[Sources/Research-Papers/Lavender Jiang - Generalist Foundation Models Are Not Clinical Enough for Hospital Operations|Lavender Jiang's ongoing work]] finds that "generalist foundation models are not clinical enough for hospital operations" — but even NYUTron only predicts static outcomes from text. [[Sources/Research-Papers/EHRSHOT An EHR Benchmark for Few-Shot Evaluation of Foundation Models|EHRSHOT]] hits the real wall: structured EHR data is fundamentally non-linguistic, and traditional ML achieves 0.847 AUROC where GPT-4 manages 0.629. Foundation models also fail at perturbation/intervention prediction — [[Sources/Browser-History/PerturBench and Foundation Model Perturbation Failures|Nature Methods (2025)]] found scGPT and Geneformer perform *worse* than taking the mean of training examples. At the top of the ladder, the question changes entirely: a clinician asks "given this patient + Drug X, what happens in 6 months?" Three independent groups have now shown JEPA-style world models outperform LLMs on exactly these dynamic tasks — [[Sources/Research-Papers/RadJEPA Radiology Encoder for Chest X-Rays via Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture|RadJEPA]] for radiology, [[Sources/Research-Papers/EchoJEPA A Latent Predictive Foundation Model for Echocardiography|EchoJEPA]] for cardiology (+20% on cardiac function, zero-shot pediatric surpassing fine-tuned baselines), and Standard Model Bio for oncology (+18% on treatment change, +8.5% on heterogeneous sarcoma).

## What This Means Going Forward

The ladder reveals that clinical AI is not one market — it is five distinct capability layers, each with different architectures, buyers, and competitive dynamics. Most healthcare AI investment has concentrated at Rungs 1-3: ambient scribes (Commure: >1% of US appointments), chatbots, triage, documentation — all LLM-native, all improving, all commoditizing as frontier models get better at conversation. Companies that win at conversation will not automatically win at prediction. Rungs 4-5 require different architectures (world models, specialized EHR foundation models), different data (longitudinal structured records + imaging + genomics, not just clinical text), and different buyers (pharma paying for trajectory prediction in trial design and treatment selection, not health systems paying per-note for documentation). The gap is structural: a [[Sources/Research-Papers/Knowledge-Practice Performance Gap in Clinical LLMs Systematic Review of 39 Benchmarks|systematic review of 39 benchmarks]] shows the 39-45 point knowledge-practice drop is consistent across model families, languages, and specialties — this is not a "GPT-5 will fix it" problem. Reasoning models (o3, chain-of-thought) may compress the gap at Rungs 2-3, but the EHRSHOT numbers at Rung 4 (traditional ML 0.847 vs GPT-4 0.629) have not moved.

The risk to this thesis is that data matters more than architecture. [[Sources/LLM-Chats/Claude-Epic Curiosity healthcare foundation model memo|Epic's Curiosity]] achieves 0.925 AUROC on chronic heart failure trajectory using standard transformers on 115B events — no JEPA required. If prediction is primarily a data problem, Epic's 300M patient moat and one-click distribution to 1,760+ hospitals makes Rung 5 startups irrelevant. There is also a blurriness at the boundary: the [[Sources/Research-Papers/NEP Framework Building the EHR Foundation Model via Next Event Prediction|NEP framework]] fine-tunes Llama-3.1 on clinical event sequences and achieves +3.5% c-index on pancreatic cancer — technically still an LLM, just with a clinical training objective. The line between "fine-tuned LLM" and "world model" is not as clean as the ladder implies. But the opportunity is that Epic is EHR-only — no imaging, no genomics, no pathology — and the Rung 5 trajectory prediction results are early but architecturally driven, not just data-driven (JEPA lifts on heterogeneous sarcoma where pattern-matching fails). The open question is whether this generalizes beyond oncology. SMB validated at MSK, [[Sources/Research-Papers/EchoJEPA A Latent Predictive Foundation Model for Echocardiography|EchoJEPA]] in cardiology, [[Sources/Research-Papers/RadJEPA Radiology Encoder for Chest X-Rays via Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture|RadJEPA]] in radiology — if the JEPA paradigm replicates across chronic disease, neurology, and mental health, the Rung 5 market is massive. If it doesn't transfer outside a single cancer center, the thesis narrows. The startups that know which rung they are building for, and why LLMs structurally cannot serve that rung, will build defensible companies. The ones pitching "AI for healthcare" without specifying the rung are building on a commodity.
