## Overview

Clinical AI evaluation follows a ladder, and each rung measures a different capability. The lower rungs like medical knowledge and patient conversations are where most benchmarks and investment dollars are today. The upper rungs where you are predicting patient outcomes and simulating disease trajectories, which require institutional data that doesn't exist on the open internet. The framework is inspired by [[Sources/Research-Papers/Miller's Pyramid Assessment of Clinical Skills Competence Performance|Miller's Pyramid (1990)]] but is different human trainees vs. AI. Models cannot predict patient trajectories without longitudinal records behind institutional walls.

Each rung in this table answers a fundamentally different question, requiring different data and different approaches, and it's not just that these are harder or easier. We can already see the transition at Rung 2 where on HealthBench's hardest clinical cases, an agentic RAG system beat raw GPT-5 by engineering better clinical retrieval.

There's no durable advantage in being the best at medical Q&A when 90 models score above 96% on USMLE, and o3 doubled GPT-4o on HealthBench in months. Context engineering (e.g., how and when you retrieve data with the right clinical framing) and access to institutional longitudinal data for fine-tuning and pre-training are what separate the winners at these different rungs as we get into real-world deployment.

## What This Means Going Forward

Most healthcare AI investment to-date ([[Sources/Market-Research-PDFs/Bessemer State of Health AI 2026|55% of all health tech funding, $14B across 527 deals]]) is concentrated at Rungs 1-3 (ambient scribes, chatbots, triage, documentation, etc) but what is defensible going forward? 
- **Domain expertise and context engineering.** MedAgentBench showed clinical AI agents jumping from 69.7% to 91-98% accuracy through prompt engineering, tool design, and memory alone. DR. INFO beat GPT-5 on HealthBench Hard the same way. The defensible position at lower rungs is clinical workflow integration and domain-specific retrieval, but this requires domain expertise to know exactly what info to pull (when, why, and how)
- **Data access for more dynamic tasks.** Architectures will evolve ([[Sources/LLM-Chats/Claude-Epic Curiosity healthcare foundation model memo|Epic's CoMET]], JEPA ([[Sources/Research-Papers/The Patient is not a Moving Document A World Model Training Paradigm for Longitudinal EHR|Standard Model Bio]]) but what matters is access to millions of longitudinal patient records locked inside health systems. Epic runs on 115B events from 118M patients across [1,760+ hospitals](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/epics-dominance-in-12-numbers/) but you don't need Epic level scale to drive value. SMB, for example, reached competitive trajectory prediction by fine-tuning on 1.2M patients (100x smaller) and their JEPA world model trained on just 36K MSK patients captures disease dynamics that autoregressive baselines miss entirely.
- **Evaluation on real-world outcomes.** Nobody is measuring performance in the real-world effectively at scale. The [[Sources/Research-Papers/ARISE State of Clinical AI Report 2026|ARISE Report (2026)]] found nearly half of 500+ medical AI studies still use exam-style questions and only 5% use real patient data. Even top-performing models make 12-15 severe errors per 100 clinical cases. **There is no [[Sources/Research-Papers/METR Model Evaluation and Threat Research|METR]] for healthcare**. No independent organization tracking clinical AI against real patient outcomes the way METR tracks AI task-completion with standardized protocols. A health system with 5 years of de-identified data can backtest any AI model against known outcomes: "if we'd deployed this radiology AI in 2022, would it have caught the cancers that were missed?" Start per-system, sell the evaluation as a procurement tool for CIOs. As you accumulate evaluations across 20, 50, 100 health systems, that dataset of matched prediction-to-outcome pairs becomes the independent evaluation layer the field is missing.
