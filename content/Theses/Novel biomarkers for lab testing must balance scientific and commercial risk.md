## Overview

Proteomics platforms are consolidating into genomics giants while the recurring revenue model for novel biomarkers remains unproven at scale. Thermo paid $3.1B for Olink (40-50% high-plex market share, 5,300+ targets) in July 2024; Illumina acquired SomaLogic for $350M+ $75M milestones (9,500 unique human proteins) closing H1 2026. The mechanism is structural: proteins change weekly/monthly/yearly, creating longitudinal monitoring needs vs. DNA's once-and-done utility. Quanterix runs 80% recurring revenue through consumables/reagents, validating the model. But execution is brutal: Quanterix revenue down 29% YoY in Q2 2025, stock down 62% in 6 months, NIH funding cuts affecting 22% of revenue. UK Biobank selected Olink for 5,400 proteins from 600,000 samples (data releases 2026-2027), proving platform scale.

The opportunity is in disease-specific recurring diagnostics where clinical utility is proven and reimbursement paths are clearer than broad panels. The 90%+ validation failure rate for discovery biomarkers creates a narrow funnel—startups must pick indications where clinical utility is already demonstrated. Abbott acquiring Exact Sciences confirms multi-cancer early detection "will be like lipid panel test every year after a certain age." Tempus stock +65% since IPO (+$5.7B market cap) at 9.3x EV/Rev with 85% revenue growth—proving AI-driven diagnostics can reach $100M ARR in <5 years vs 10+ years for prior healthcare software. The buyers are longevity clinics (Noom Proactive Health at $149/mo with biomarkers every 4 months), health systems (Labcorp Whole Health: 1,000+ biomarkers, 2,200+ collection sites), and pharma (UK Biobank, Regeneron studies). Window is 2-3 years before incumbents integrate proteomics into genomics stacks.

## Bull Case

**Strategic M&A validates market at multi-billion scale.** Thermo paid $3.1B for Olink at 40-50% market share (July 2024). Illumina acquired SomaLogic for $350M+ $75M milestones (closed H1 2026). Olink now "proteomic science business" within Thermo. SomaLogic integrated into Illumina's multiomics strategy to "scale that technology faster, expand customer adoption."
> "Our Olink technology was selected by the U.K. Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project to support the world's largest human proteomic study of its kind." — Marc Casper, Thermo Fisher CEO, Q4 2024

**Recurring revenue model proven in diagnostics.** Quanterix runs 80% recurring revenue through consumables/reagents despite 29% revenue decline in Q2 2025. The mechanism is structural: proteins change weekly/monthly/yearly, creating longitudinal monitoring needs vs. DNA's once-and-done utility. Noom's Proactive Health tests biomarkers every 4 months; Labcorp launched 1,000+ biomarker panels for recurring wellness testing.
> "Proteins change constantly vs. DNA 'once and done.' TAM ~$100B, 5-10% penetration. Tens of dollars per sample." — Industry operator

**Institutional adoption at unprecedented scale.** UK Biobank selected Olink for 5,400 proteins from 600,000 samples (Q1 2025 start, data releases 2026-2027). Regeneron selected Olink Explore HT for 200,000 patient samples from Geisinger Health Study. This validates platforms for population-scale research and creates massive longitudinal datasets.
> "We will scale that technology faster, expand customer adoption, and achieve greater operational efficiencies." — Jacob Thaysen, Illumina CEO on SomaLogic acquisition

**Sensitivity breakthroughs enabling new clinical use cases.** Alamar NULISA achieves 10,000-fold signal-to-noise improvement with attomolar detection (10-100x vs. Olink/SomaLogic). Beckman Coulter launched Access BD-tau assay for Alzheimer's research—"industry's first fully automated research use-only immunoassay for brain-derived tau protein." Blood-based biomarkers replacing invasive tissue sampling.
> "Beckman launched the Access BD-tau assay... to help improve research efficiency, enhance consistency in long-term clinical trials and support accelerated regulatory pathways." — Danaher Q3 2025

## Bear Case

**Poster child struggling significantly.** Quanterix revenue down 29% YoY in Q2 2025. Stock down 62% in 6 months. NIH funding cuts affecting 22% of revenue. Akoya integration adding complexity. Analyst ratings mostly "Hold" with target prices $7.50-$18. Path to cash flow breakeven pushed to 2026.
> "Quanterix experienced financial pressures evidenced by a revenue decline in Q2 2025 and an increased adjusted EBITDA loss, signaling operational and market headwinds." — Quanterix Q3 2025 Results

**Mass spec costs declining faster than expected.** "$10 proteome" research achieved with Orbitrap Astral and timsTOF Ultra 2. Mean proteomics test cost ~$897/patient but falling. LC-MS/MS at $250/sample academic, $100/injection for targeted panels. Could erode affinity platform advantage if mass spec achieves clinical workflow integration.

**90%+ validation failure rate creates narrow funnel.** Discovery biomarkers fail clinical validation at >90% rate. MolDX requires Technical Assessment, Z-Codes for billing. Panels with 15+ targets face denials. CPT 87801 reimburses ~$75 (below actual cost). PromarkerD received $390.75 Medicare PLA code—still far below platform costs. The gap between what can be measured (11,000+ proteins) and what has clinical utility is vast.

**Reimbursement remains challenging.** Broad proteomics panels struggle with payer coverage. Disease-specific tests like LucentAD priced at $897 recommendation, but uptake depends on clinical guidelines. "So what" problem persists—novel biomarkers can be measured but clinical action unclear.

**What would have to be true for you to be wrong?** Three scenarios challenge this thesis. First, mass spec achieves <$100/sample with <2hrs hands-on time in clinical settings—affinity platforms lose sensitivity advantage. Second, proteomics reimbursement stays stuck at <$100 per test outside specialty applications—recurring model never scales. Third, UK Biobank and Regeneron datasets become public goods, eliminating data moat for platforms. In any scenario, vertical diagnostics startups struggle to reach profitability before incumbents close the gap.

## Startup Opportunities

**1. Disease-Specific Recurring Proteomics Diagnostics (Stage: Emerging)**
- Why: Proteomics enables recurring monitoring (proteins change, DNA doesn't) but broad panels face "so what" problem. Disease-specific tests where clinical utility is proven have clearer path.
- Wedge: Build Quanterix-model vertical for single chronic disease (diabetic kidney disease, heart failure, autoimmune) with clear longitudinal monitoring utility. Partner with CCRCs/longevity clinics.
- The edge: Indication-specific reimbursement path is clearer than broad panels; recurring revenue from monitoring vs. one-shot diagnosis. Target $897+ reimbursement per test.
- Buyers: Specialty clinics, CCRCs, longevity medicine practices
- Risk: Reimbursement never materializes outside specialty; incumbents add disease-specific modules

**2. Proteomics Standardization & QC Platform (Stage: Early)**
- Why: Standardization identified as #1 barrier. OSPP (Charite Open Standard for Plasma Proteomics) released 2025 but needs commercial implementation.
- Wedge: Build QC reagents + reference standards for clinical trials; software for cross-platform data normalization.
- The edge: Neutral arbiter position; 90%+ validation failure rates create urgent need; regulatory mandate could accelerate demand.
- Buyers: Pharma clinical trials, academic research cores, lab networks
- Risk: Incumbents (Thermo, Illumina) bundle standardization into platforms

**3. Autoimmune Precision Platform (Stage: Emerging)**
- Why: "Russian roulette of medications" is current standard for RA/lupus. Biologics fail 30-40% first-line at $30K-100K/year. No Tempus-equivalent exists in autoimmune. Synovial signatures can predict TNF response but not yet in clinical practice.
- Wedge: Treatment selection + monitoring + pharma data licensing (70%+ margin on data). Build largest autoimmune multiomics database.
- The edge: Autoimmune diagnostics underserved ($5.7B market); single-product competitors exist but no platform; rheumatology concentrated (500 high-volume practices = 80% volume).
- Buyers: Rheumatologists, payers (desperate to avoid biologic waste), pharma (needs RWD on drug response)
- Risk: Competitors have head start; Exagen entrenched in diagnosis; specialty channel is slow

## Open Questions

**Will mass spec break into routine clinical workflow by 2028?** Stakes: "$10 proteome" research exists but requires Orbitrap Astral/timsTOF Ultra 2 instruments. If mass spec achieves <$100/sample with automated interpretation, affinity platforms (Olink, SomaLogic) lose sensitivity advantage. Watch: FDA clearance of clinical mass spec assays, Thermo/Danaher clinical diagnostics moves.

**Can any proteomics company achieve >$500M revenue with >70% recurring by 2028?** Stakes: Proves recurring revenue model at scale. Quanterix struggling; UK Biobank/Regeneron studies validate platform but not commercial viability. This is the key test of whether mechanism translates to sustainable business.

**Will CMS establish reimbursement codes for longitudinal biomarker monitoring?** Stakes: Unlocks recurring revenue model at scale. Currently only cancer MRD has clear monitoring reimbursement. If CMS creates pathway for chronic disease biomarker monitoring, thesis accelerates. Watch: Abbott/Exact Sciences integration, Tempus Medicare coverage expansion.
