## Overview
Incumbents price healthcare risk at the population level because their business model depends on it. Startups that price at the individual level and can guarantee the number capture the entire spread. This model becomes more valuable, not less, as AI commoditizes the rest of the stack.
Healthcare incumbents (carriers, PBMs, legacy MGAs/MGUs, etc) typically price risk at the population level. They pool members into blunt demographic buckets (age, geography, employer size, specialty) and charge a rate that covers the average. XX - 1 sentence here.

This isn't a failure of intelligence. It's the business model. Carriers profit from opacity: the less the buyer understands the true cost of their risk, the wider the carrier's margin. Population pooling also stabilizes reserve requirements and simplifies regulatory compliance. It is the rational strategy for an incumbent with a \$500B book of business.
But a new entrant willing to do the work — aggregating claims data incumbents won't share, building repricing engines incumbents won't build, and guaranteeing a number incumbents won't offer — can price at the individual level: member by member, drug by drug, episode by episode.

## The Stack: Why Layer Matters
We see three layers where AI enters healthcare risk. Most companies build at the wrong one.
![]()
**Layer 1 — Dashboards.** ACO analytics, MLR decomposition, denial pattern visualization. Useful but commoditized. The intelligence sits in a pane of glass that a human must act on, and the vendor captures \$5-15 PMPM regardless of whether anyone does. Incumbents (Milliman, Optum Advisory) already operate here. *In WriteWise terms: this is the equivalent of giving an employer a report showing their PBM is overcharging them. Interesting, not valuable.*
**Layer 2 — Workflow Integration.** Embedding predictions into operational decisions in real time. WriteWise's MavRx platform sits here — it ingests claims data, reprices across a panel of PBMs, and uses predictive algorithms to forecast true forward-looking net cost for each employer group. Other examples: Bluevia does 10-second perioperative risk scoring on 84,875 preoperative notes (+38.3% AUROC vs traditional models), Stream does AI-powered treatment determinations in workers comp UR. All require clinical trust and system access, all more defensible than dashboards. But Layer 2 alone doesn't capture the spread. *In WriteWise terms: MavRx is critical infrastructure — but the value compounds only when it feeds the guarantee.*
**Layer 3 — Underwriting.** WriteWise guarantees total pharmacy cost at a fixed PMPM, takes the risk itself, and captures the \$12.2M spread with sub-10% loss ratios. This is where the company makes money. Sparx does the same in ACO risk — \$1B+ reinsurance capacity, one contract realized 62.1% savings. Indigo prescores every US physician and auto-underwrites 20% of malpractice policies. Carrum bundles surgical episodes, guarantees a price, delivers 0.8% knee readmission vs 2.7% national average — the readmission delta IS the underwriting edge. *The question for every deal: does this company bear financial risk on its predictions? If not, it's not underwriting — it's a dashboard with better data.*
**The critical insight: control of the lower layers feeds the underwriting.** If you're confident in AI-driven patient steerage to optimize the formulary, you can underwrite more aggressively. If you're automating AWVs and collecting timely data, you can underwrite payment acceleration. The engagement layer becomes an input to the risk model, not a cost center bolted onto it. The companies that own both the pricing model AND the operational machinery that produces the predicted outcome will compound advantages. Neither works without the other. Briggs: "patient selection (underwriting) + post-procedure compliance" are the two differentiators — AI collapses them into one system.

## Where This Repeats
We should be actively sourcing founders running the WriteWise playbook in these markets:
**Surgical Episodes** — The clearest next application. Carrum proves bundled-episode pricing works for hip/knee. The real unlock: AI bakes engagement into the underwriting model itself. A surgery bundler that automates post-op follow-ups, RPM tracking, medication adherence, and escalation triggers isn't just delivering care — it's producing the data that makes the underwriting model more precise. Every automated touchpoint that catches a complication early directly reduces the variance the model prices for. 29.3M adults eligible for bariatric surgery but \<1% get it — massive latent demand suppressed by cost uncertainty that tighter underwriting could unlock. Bariatric, cardiology, and spine all moving outpatient with the same model: engage member → right provider → follow-up → underwrite the episode.
**Dental/Vision** — \$140B dental market, expense ratios 80-90%. Soris is attempting this as a full carrier, but the MGU model (bear underwriting risk without carrier overhead) is more capital-efficient. LightSpun proves AI adjudication works at Layer 2. The Layer 3 move is underwriting dental episode cost with the same guarantee structure.
**Workers Comp** — \$55B market, 25% admin overhead. Actuarial models price on historical claim rates but miss behavioral risk factors driving 80%+ of outcomes. Shift Health validated 35% claim reduction with continuous behavioral coaching. Nobody combines predictive risk selection + integrated intervention + takes the underwriting risk. WriteWise's own product roadmap lists workers comp as an expansion target.
**Stop-Loss for Self-Funded Employers** — Adjacent to WriteWise's pharmacy carve-out. Nationwide paid \$1.25B for Allstate's book because legacy pricing is blunt group demographics. AI-native stop-loss MGUs can price at individual level. Loss ratios deteriorated from 78.5% to 84.1% — the market is getting harder for legacy underwriters, easier for AI-native ones.
**Disability** — \$142B market. Return-to-work probability drops to \~50% after 6 months, below \~20% after 12. 10-15% of claims drive majority of cost. DisabilityIQ is building the temporal modeling engine at Layer 2 today. The Layer 3 move: underwrite early intervention ROI.
**Preventive Care** — The frontier Briggs flagged. No company takes actuarial risk on preventive care outcomes. If you can underwrite the claim that a preventive intervention prevents a future cost, you've created a new insurance product. Furthest out, but structurally the most interesting.

## Sourcing Filter
What makes a WriteWise-shaped opportunity:
1. **Actuarial credibility in the founder.** Hari had Verikai (\$170M exit) and Radion. The reinsurance market is relationship-driven and credentialist. A first-time founder with no actuarial background won't get capacity.
2. **Standardizable unit of risk.** Pharmacy works because every drug is an SKU. Surgical episodes work because CPT codes are standardized. Workers comp is messier — behavioral risk factors aren't codified. The more standardized the unit, the faster the model proves out.
3. **Reinsurance access without 3-5 years of claims history.** WriteWise solved this via SiriusPoint and captive structures. Pearl Health spent 1+ year finding ACO reinsurance. The reinsurance bottleneck kills more underwriting startups than model quality does.
4. **Structural misalignment in the incumbent business model.** PBMs profit from opacity — they can't price at NDC level AND choose not to because opacity is profitable. CVS's PBM market-basket guarantee misalignment caused a \$240M guidance reduction. If the incumbent could easily reprice at the same resolution, the arbitrage isn't durable.

## Risks
**The proof cycle is the primary kill risk.** AI underwriting models need 3-5 years of claims data to demonstrate sustained profitability. Reinsurers won't back unproven models. The arbitrage exists but capturing it requires surviving long enough to prove you can.
**Tail risk is accelerating faster than models can be proven.** Gene therapy cases at one major reinsurer went from 2 to 17 in a single year. GLP-1 PMPM grew at 77% CAGR from \$1.43 (2019) to \$24.59 (2024). The underwriting edge is perishable — models must be continuously recalibrated.
**Aggregation could win again at the underwriting layer.** If a carrier builds or acquires equivalent AI underwriting and prices at the same granularity while keeping distribution advantage, the MGU model gets squeezed. This bundling cycle has happened before in healthcare. But the business model misalignment argument says they won't — population pooling is how carriers make money.
**This thesis breaks if** a major incumbent demonstrates it can price at individual/group level and still maintain its core economics. Watch for: UNH's \$1B AI-efficiency target, carrier acquisitions of actuarial AI companies, and whether legacy reinsurers start offering capacity to AI-native MGUs (which would validate the model but also lower barriers to entry).
