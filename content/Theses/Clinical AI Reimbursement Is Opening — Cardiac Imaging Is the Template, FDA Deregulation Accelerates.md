# Clinical AI Reimbursement Is Opening — Cardiac Imaging Is the Template, FDA Deregulation Accelerates

## Overview

Medicare paying $950-$1,017 per AI cardiac CT interpretation (CPT 75577, effective January 2026) is the largest AI-for-diagnosis payer validation in healthcare history—5x more than the scan itself. This reimbursement covers 93.6 million Medicare beneficiaries. The mechanism is now proven: FDA 510(k) clearance, CPT code application, MAC coverage determination, private payer follow-on. Cardiac imaging completed this sequence in approximately 3 years. Four FDA-cleared platforms now compete for this market: Cleerly (plaque features with clinical event validation), HeartFlow (fluid dynamics and 3D reconstruction with event validation), Elucid (plaque composition via histology), and Caristo (fat attenuation index measuring arterial inflammation—the only technology linked to cardiac mortality in a 40,000-patient, 10-year UK study showing 1 inflamed artery = 13x death risk). Private payers followed within months: Aetna, UHC, Cigna, and Humana cover 86M+ commercial lives as of October 2025-January 2026.

Simultaneously, FDA under Commissioner Makary is loosening oversight through guidance-level changes that accelerate clinical AI deployment. January 2026 guidance exempts wellness devices measuring blood pressure and blood glucose from premarket review. Clinical decision support software providing "sole recommendations" can now be exempt—reversing the WHOOP warning letter approach. A petition to exempt radiology AI from 510(k) is under FDA review. Real-world data acceptance expanding: only 35 applications included RWD since 2016, but Makary announced "you can submit whatever real-world data you want now." The Health Tech Investment Act (S.1399) proposes statutory 5-year guaranteed reimbursement for FDA-cleared AI devices. However, the window is time-bounded: guidance-level changes can reverse with the next administration. Companies that lock in FDA clearance, CPT codes, and payer contracts during 2025-2028 build durable position. Companies relying on enforcement discretion face whiplash risk.

## Bull Case

**Medicare pays $950-$1,017 per AI interpretation (5x the scan itself).** CPT 75577 for cardiac AI plaque analysis achieved Category I status effective January 1, 2026, covering all 93.6M Medicare beneficiaries. 5 of 7 MACs have approved coverage. This represents the highest per-procedure AI reimbursement in healthcare.

**Private payer coverage achieved dominant scale rapidly.** Aetna, UHC, Cigna, and Humana provide coverage effective October 2025 through January 2026, reaching 86M+ commercial lives. The speed of private payer follow-on validates the template: once Medicare moves, commercial payers follow within months.

**FDA wellness and CDS exemptions materially expand what's deployable without premarket review.** Commissioner Makary at CES January 2026 announced blood pressure and blood glucose readings can be wellness features exempt from medical device regulation. Clinical decision support software with "sole medical recommendation" can now be exempt—previously such software would have been regulated as a device. RWD barriers removed.

**S.1399 would codify 5-year guaranteed reimbursement into statute.** The Health Tech Investment Act creates "New Technology Ambulatory Payment Classification" under OPPS for algorithm-based healthcare services. Minimum 5-year separate reimbursement before transition to standard classifications. Bipartisan sponsorship (Rounds R-SD, Heinrich D-NM). 600+ FDA-authorized AI devices as of April 2025.

## Bear Case

**Clinical evidence gaps remain for cardiac AI—prediction is not intervention.** UK NHS FFR-CT deployment across 90,553 patients showed reduced invasive procedures but no significant mortality reduction. DANCAVAS II screened 31,268 men 60-64 and found increased severe bleeding without mortality reduction. More than 99% of cardiac event patients had uncontrolled standard risk factors before. The fundamental critique: these tools predict which plaques look risky, not whether acting on predictions prevents heart attacks.

**All guidance-level FDA changes are reversible on day one.** Wellness exemptions, CDS exemptions, and RWD acceptance were published without notice-and-comment rulemaking. A 2028 election producing a pro-regulation administration could reverse them overnight. S.1399 has only 40-50% passage probability. Companies relying on enforcement discretion rather than statutory protection face binary reversal risk.

**9.4% of FDA-cleared AI devices have been recalled.** 182 recall events primarily due to diagnostic or measurement errors. 25% of safety reports potentially related to AI/ML issues. 510(k)-cleared devices with only functional equivalence (no clinical validation) concentrated among recalls. A high-profile patient harm event could trigger procurement freezes and political backlash.

**What would have to be true for you to be wrong?**

Two paths break this thesis. First, the window never opens meaningfully: S.1399 fails, FDA guidance gets watered down before implementation, CMS does not create new clinical AI payment codes beyond cardiac. By mid-2027, no second clinical AI category achieves Category I CPT code status following the cardiac playbook—proving cardiac was the exception, not the template.

Second, the window opens but slams shut: a high-profile AI patient safety event (diagnostic error leading to death or disability) creates political backlash. The 2028 election reverses Makary-era guidance. CMS restricts rather than expands clinical AI reimbursement citing cost or outcomes concerns. Companies that rushed to market without building durable regulatory infrastructure face sudden exposure.

## Startup Opportunities

**Clinical AI Reimbursement Infrastructure (Stage: Emerging)**
Every clinical AI company must navigate FDA clearance, CPT code application, MAC coverage determinations, and commercial billing. Cardiac imaging took 3 years to complete this sequence. Most startups have no reimbursement expertise—they hire ex-payer consultants at $500/hour after the fact.
- **Wedge:** Start with cardiac imaging billing (known playbook, proven codes). Help Cleerly/HeartFlow competitors navigate coverage.
- **Expansion:** Pathology AI (Category III codes only, next for Category I), radiology AI (510(k) exemption petition pending).
- **The edge:** First-mover in "Stripe for clinical AI billing" before categories proliferate. S.1399 passage would dramatically expand addressable market.
- **Buyers:** AI diagnostic companies (Cleerly, HeartFlow, Aidoc, Viz.ai), health systems implementing AI tools.
- **Risk:** Epic builds this internally. CMS creates simplified pathway reducing need for navigation.

**Regulatory-Durable Consumer Health Devices—Cuffless BP (Stage: Emerging)**
FDA draft guidance (January 2026) creates clear clinical pathway for cuffless non-invasive BP devices. 116M US adults have hypertension. Devices with FDA clearance survive political cycles; wellness-only devices relying on Makary-era guidance don't.
- **Wedge:** Target continuous monitoring for hypertension management (clinical grade, not wellness). Integrate with RPM reimbursement codes (CPT 99453-99458).
- **The edge:** Invest in clinical-grade validation now while competitors rely on wellness exemptions. Apple Watch BP features will push category—be the clinical-grade alternative.
- **Buyers:** Cardiology practices, hypertension clinics, value-based care organizations.
- **Risk:** Wellness exemption proves durable, eliminating clinical differentiation advantage. Apple captures clinical-grade market directly.

**RWD Infrastructure for Regulatory Submissions (Stage: Proven)**
FDA's RWD acceptance expansion creates demand for quality infrastructure. But raw data submission without quality layer is useless—computable phenotypes, validation, and audit trails still required.
- **Wedge:** Build on Sentinel Common Data Model (statutory via FDAAA 2007, not guidance-based). Sentinel covers 138.7M members and influenced 120+ regulatory actions.
- **The edge:** Statutory foundation survives administration changes; guidance-based competitors face reversal risk. Datavant (500+ institutions, 60M records) shows scale is achievable.
- **Buyers:** Pharma R&D (trials with RWE component), AI diagnostic companies (post-market surveillance), CROs.
- **Risk:** Pharma doesn't actually adopt RWD at scale; traditional trials remain default despite FDA encouragement.

## Open Questions

**Will CMS sustain $1,000+ cardiac AI reimbursement despite weak mortality data?** If yes, clinical AI reimbursement follows technology-adoption pattern (pay for capability, not outcomes) rather than evidence-based-medicine pattern. Every other clinical AI category has a clear playbook. If CMS reduces rates after 2-3 years citing lack of outcomes data, the template breaks.
Stakes: Determines whether cardiac is exception or template.

**Does a second clinical AI category achieve Category I CPT code by end of 2027?** Radiology AI 510(k) exemption petition is under FDA review—if granted, dramatically reduces regulatory burden for AI radiology deployment. Pathology AI has Category III codes only. Ophthalmology (diabetic retinopathy) has dedicated CPT codes but limited adoption.
Stakes: Proves the template is replicable, not a one-off cardiac anomaly.

**Will S.1399 pass before the 2028 election?** 40-50% probability. Bipartisan sponsors (Rounds R, Heinrich D), AdvaMed support, companion House bill (H.R. 6197). But CBO scoring could show short-term Medicare cost increase creating opposition. If passes, codifies reimbursement architecture into statute.
Stakes: Statutory protection vs. guidance-level fragility for entire clinical AI category.
